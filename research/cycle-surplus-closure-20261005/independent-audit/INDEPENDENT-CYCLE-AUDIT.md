## 2. Exact first-cluster and graph proof

Root a center spanning tree across a chosen center edge. Each other center uses its own physical leaf as its selected child. Collapse the four-force root spine and each two-force side spine, conditional on the entire old query bank and the entire auxiliary Gaussian bank.

A reverse monomial corresponds exactly to a connected, ancestor-closed set of centers containing the two root-spine centers. If a child side spine is not inserted, its independent centered reverse Gaussian appears once at its parent's chunk. Consequently every incomplete monomial has zero expectation already over side reverse Gaussians. The complete monomial contains every center and physical leaf once; each opened-edge Gaussian then appears exactly twice and closes the intended edge. No graph-theoretic condition on the complement of the spanning tree is needed for this centering proof.

In a product of root-spine monomials every retained center has its own C0 leaf. Its four other slots are either retained center-tree edges or Gaussian boundary slots. No Gaussian color occurs twice at one center occurrence. Wick pairing therefore joins distinct center occurrences and preserves center degree four; repeated original query labels never identify distinct occurrences. Each monomial is connected before Wick edges. The moment-cumulant subtraction retains exactly diagrams connecting the monomial groups. Thus connected offspring have the claimed connected loopless 4-regular multigraph form, with one permanent physical leaf per center.

The short-root-spine choice, rather than a longest-path convention, is essential to preservation of that permanent leaf.

## 3. One-Hilbert argument

The tensor of a center plus its C0 leaf has one uncontracted physical slot, four center-boundary slots and all proper-cut bounds. Contracting an attached chunk with the existing connected group across all their shared edges is ordinary multiplication after flattening those contracted slots. It cannot create a free self-trace. Its full HS norm is at most the existing HS norm times the attached chunk's proper-cut norm, because the new chunk retains its physical slot. Repetition gives one sqrt(D), rather than one per center or cycle.

For proper-cut control, write a desired bipartition of the remaining slots of two groups and use row-column operator Cauchy-Schwarz on their shared boundary indices. At least one admissible orientation leaves nonempty free slots on the required sides, because both groups retain a physical slot and the output cut is proper. Each Gram factor is then a proper flattening of one group. This proves the proper-cut induction as well. Finally, isolating one D-dimensional physical index recovers the full-HS sqrt(D) bound directly.

This argument is for the connected coefficient diagrams. It must not be applied indiscriminately to individual forward covariance-root square contractions, whose internal endpoint indices may have been consumed. Whole-matrix operator/frame bounds handle those polynomial terms instead.

## 4. Exact scaling and finite queue

For n centers, the product of one root amplitude alpha^(d+beta)H, n-1 ordinary leaf amplitudes alpha^beta and n center amplitudes alpha^beta z is alpha^(2 beta n+d)H z^n.

An h-cluster contains h intact root spines and j inserted side spines. Thus n'=2h+j and A'=h(d+4 beta)+2 beta j=2 beta n'+h d. Every nonmain cluster has h>=2, so d'=h d>=2d. The effective exponent is (2 beta-gamma)n'+h d.

The retained queue is finite when beta>gamma>0, 2 beta-gamma>0 and d0>0: d<P and n<P/(2 beta-gamma) for every retained coefficient, the depth is bounded by the number of doublings below P, and h is bounded by P/d. Effective exponent itself need not be monotone. It is valid to leave a high-exponent emitted current unexecuted; executing a repair of it could expose lower-exponent offspring and is a different operation.

At beta=1/2, gamma=1/3, initial n=4,d=4, the first h=2 root-only term has n'=4,A'=12 and exponent 12-4/3=32/3. The nonroot native radius is at most a public-log factor times alpha^(1/6), so arbitrary fixed finite prior accuracy can be obtained by a sufficiently high fixed pair order.

## 6. Law-level completion gate and a sufficient frame induction

The original frozen Section 7 does not by itself give a full same-endpoint remainder proof: bounded Taylor truncation plus formal cumulant cancellation is not enough. The following is the required conditional extension of LOW30's polynomial and same-endpoint argument.

Fix the entire original bank, clock data, shields, coefficient factors and readout shares. Write t=alpha^(1/6) and zbar=tau z. Along the comparison homotopy hold zbar and every other recorded datum fixed. The local amplitudes become t zbar at centers, t^3 at nonroot leaves and t^(6d+3)H at roots. Their exponents are positive integers. The effective coefficient cutoff P is an ordinary finite t-degree cutoff at 6P, up to rounding and separately retained equality cases.

Work with exact collapsed selected spines, and then with their finite polynomial expansions. A vector coefficient is a finite sum A Z, where Z is that spine's terminal or completion innovation, independent of A. Distinct side subtrees have distinct such private innovations even though their matrix factors share auxiliary G.

For every matrix coefficient A retain its operator moments and all three proper flattenings of its derivative tensor D_xi A, where xi is the full auxiliary/reset Gaussian block. In coordinates B_j=partial_(xi_j) A, these are:

- the row frame norm ||sum_j B_j B_j^T||op^(1/2);
- the transposed row frame norm ||sum_j B_j^T B_j||op^(1/2);
- the HS-input norm ||D_xi A : ell2(xi) -> HS||op.

The last is essential. The two Gaussian-series frames for D_xi(A Z) are the row frame and the HS-input norm, not just the two row frames. Native all-proper-cut control supplies these local source flattenings.

Multilinear side substitution is handled one independent side innovation at a time. Conditional on shared G and the other private variables, substitute B Z and apply the rectangular Gaussian-series estimate in Z. Transport its frame by ||B||op and remove that factor using fixed Holder. The other sibling coefficients do not observe Z. In a derivative side hit, compose the local derivative-frame map with D_xi Y_child; each of its three flattenings is bounded by the corresponding local frame times ||D_xi Y_child||op. The side derivative removes that slot by multilinearity. Shared-G derivatives are sums of local and recursive side hits, with Holder rather than a false independence assertion.

Matrix products and covariance-root polynomial powers preserve all three frames by the product rule, ordinary left/right operator bounds and Holder. Transposes exchange the row frames and preserve the HS-input norm. For example sum_j B_j C C^T B_j^T is bounded above by ||C||op^2 sum_j B_j B_j^T. No individual cyclic contraction of a root-square term is claimed to be an executable marked graph.

Finally E||A Z||^p has one sqrt(D) from Z, and D_xi(A Z) has operator moments from the row and HS-input frames and the independent Z. The derivative in Z itself is A. This closes the finite coefficient energy and full Gaussian-bank Jacobian induction without an additional dimension factor. The numbers of coefficients, derivative hits and Holder exponents are finite at the selected cutoff. Repeated side innovations inside matrix powers are handled through whole-factor Holder bounds, not by declaring the repetitions independent.
