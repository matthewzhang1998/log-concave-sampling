## 9. A concrete failure of the proposed recursive preservation rule

Already the first eight-force trace in Section 6 contains four unmarked C3 centers a,b,c,d, each initially attached to one physical endpoint leaf. Its root-spine edges are ab and cd; its side-noise pairings are ac and bd. Choose the legitimate G Wick matching (ab,cd) and the legitimate H matching (ac,bd). This graph is a positive, nonzero summand in the auxiliary average of the trace family. It is not removed by connected cumulant subtraction at that leading force grade. In scalar dimension it contributes to the positive coefficient 9/2.

To use the same edge-opening rule again, any spanning tree chooses one copy of each edge in an underlying four-center path. Its longest spine from a chosen physical root visits three or four centers (four when the root is attached to an endpoint center), and the two endpoint leaves. The other physical leaves are side calls. In its zero-side-cross conditional feedback those side FORCE occurrences disappear, leaving only the two endpoint physical marks. Each kept center edge has a parallel edge that was cut. The two ends of that auxiliary color therefore both lie in any chunk that contains the kept center edge.

Consequently a connected chunk with at most one occurrence of each auxiliary color can contain at most one center. There are at least three centers on that spine but only two surviving physical endpoint marks. No partition into the admitted marked chunks can cover this next spine. Equivalently at least one initial chunk has a cut-edge self-contraction or no surviving physical mark. The check enumerates all 32 labelled spanning trees, all 384 directed paths between distinct physical leaves, and all 128 longest paths from a chosen physical root. All longest-root paths fail the sufficient chunk test.

Shorter spines do not give an immediate recursive rescue. All 192 two-center paths pass the chunk test, but their root spine has four forces, so its first zero-side conditional variance again has EIGHT forces, equal to the input. Under the balanced per-occurrence normalization, the input has weight alpha^8 hatomega^4 z_c^4, while that variance has weight alpha^8 hatomega^8 z_c^4, up to known coefficients and nonzero Gaussian moments. Thus their certified alpha/center-width grade is the same 8-4 gamma; the extra unrelated-clock weight is not a guaranteed alpha gain. The 192 paths containing three or four centers have strict force-count gain, but fail the sufficient chunk certificate. The graph therefore demonstrates a precise tradeoff: this simple reopening policy cannot inherit BOTH its advertised mark-preserving certificate and a strict force/effective-grade gain on every descendant. It does not exclude same-grade coefficient inversion, another amplitude allocation or a stronger grouped kernel.

The failure is specific and limited: the earlier sufficient one-Hilbert/auxiliary closure certificate is not preserved under its longest-spine return. It does NOT establish a dimension blowup for the actual original-gradient tensor, a sign obstruction, or impossibility of a grouped kernel that evaluates the internal double-edge contraction with a new certificate.
