## 2. Exact conditional and auxiliary return

With all known signs/readout factors absorbed into lambda,u,v, the exact formal reverse symbol at fixed (q,G,H) is

    V(theta) = lambda (S+u)^T B(theta;G,H)(T+v).

S,T are independent standard side-output Gaussians. B has physical degree two and degree two in **each** auxiliary color. Lambda has four force amplitudes; u,v each contain one leaf force amplitude and one physical mark. The Gaussian bilinear determinant/resolvent identity gives exactly:

- Trace rings, h>=1: `lambda^(2h) tr[(B B^T)^h]/(2h)`, force/rank `(8h,4h)`.
- One-side terms, h>=1: `lambda^(2h) u^T(B B^T)^h u/2`, and the v analogue, force/rank `(8h+2,4h+2)`.
- Both-side terms, h>=0: `lambda^(2h+1) u^T B(B^T B)^h v`, force/rank `(8h+6,4h+4)`.

The h=0 both-side term is the desired six-force/four-mark coefficient after averaging G,H. The first conditional nonleading forces are eight, ten, and fourteen. Reversing the amplitude sign reverses the main but not an even trace ring. Setting side crosses to zero leaves the eight-force ring.

Auxiliary integration is `log E_(G,H) exp(F_(G,H))`, not `E F`. Its first new pure auxiliary covariance is force twelve. A main/ring auxiliary covariance starts at force fourteen. At any fixed cutoff, ordinary finite cumulants of the displayed polynomial coefficients give the full exact list, with the same old roots and clock labels.

For an independently checked scalar fixture

    V = s alpha^4 t theta^2 G^2 H^2 (S+p alpha theta)(T+q alpha theta),

where s,p,q are fixed scalars, the first terms of the complete joint log are

    s p q alpha^6 t theta^4
    + (9/2) s^2 alpha^8 t^2 theta^4
    + (9/2) s^2(p^2+q^2) alpha^10 t^2 theta^6
    + 4 s^2 p^2 q^2 alpha^12 t^2 theta^8
    + 333 s^3 p q alpha^14 t^3 theta^8 + ... .

Here `Var(G^2 H^2)=8`, and its third cumulant is 200. The coefficient 333 includes both the conditional fourteen-force term and the main/ring auxiliary mixture. These are finite formal jets; no ordinary real MGF existence is assumed for the unbounded polynomial target.

## 3. Correct normalization and complete actual first ledger

Write the literal product quadrature weight as

    omega = w_c hat_omega,
    z_c = w_c/sigma_c^3,
    t = omega^2/sigma_c^6 = hat_omega^2 z_c^2.

Here w_c is the one old primitive center-clock weight. All eight other clock weights of the five-star history remain in hat_omega. For dyadic endpoint panel size Delta, `w_c <= C Delta` and `sigma_c^2` is comparable to `Delta+tau^2`. Finite within-panel node counts and inverse readout shares are absorbed only into declared public-log factors Lambda.

**Preferred balanced-center amplitudes:**

    rho_L = alpha hat_omega^2,
    rho_C = -zeta alpha z_c,
    rho_D = alpha z_c,
    rho_R = rho_M = rho_N = alpha.

The fixed normalization zeta includes the requested magnitude/sign and known readout constants. Their product is exactly `-zeta alpha^6 omega^2/sigma_c^6`. Thus the leading target is unchanged.

The basic endpoint budgets are

    max z_c, sum z_c <= Lambda tau^-1,
    sum z_c/sigma_c <= Lambda tau^-2,
    sum z_c^2 <= Lambda tau^-2,
    sum z_c^2/sigma_c <= Lambda tau^-3.

Consequently the two center source radii are at most `Lambda alpha/tau`, while the root and leaves have radius at most `Lambda alpha`. Every source must be checked against its actual fixed-order small-radius threshold; alpha alone is not the center's source parameter.

The strict ancestor paths give the following center injections into the root output:

    C own original-caller first <= Lambda alpha^2 hat_omega^2 z_c/sigma_c,
    D own original-caller first <= Lambda alpha^3 hat_omega^2 z_c^2/sigma_c.

G,H inject at those same nonroot centers without an extra original inverse shield. Other original callers retain their own squared primitive weight in hat_omega, so their endpoint score sums are summable. Root and descendant-carrier paths must also be counted: a nonroot Gaussian carrier has radius one, not its local source radius. Their relevant factors are `rho_L`, `rho_L rho_C`, and `rho_L rho_C rho_D`; these are bounded by the same ledger or smaller terms.

The complete summed first budget is therefore

    Lambda [alpha + alpha^2 tau^-2 + alpha^3 tau^-3].

It is O(Lambda alpha) when `tau >= sqrt(alpha)` (alpha,tau<=1), including the proposed `tau=alpha^(1/3)`. All shared q,G,H paths are added. This is an actual finite-source/native-path bound, not a derivative of a law estimate. At tau=alpha^(1/3), the largest center radius is `Lambda alpha^(2/3)`.

**Rejected bare-root variant.** Taking `rho_L=alpha` and moving all of t to C leaves the root C0 adapter's own caller estimate `Lambda alpha/sigma_L` without its leaf clock factor. An alpha/tau term is then present in the complete available ledger. The descendant bound alpha^2/tau^3 cannot replace it. The admissible scalar source `g(x)=A x/2+A sin(kx)/(4k)` illustrates the underlying scale: its smoothed C0 coefficient is `M(q)=1/2+exp(-(sigma_L k)^2/2)cos(kq)/4`; at k=1/sigma_L its caller derivative has size c/sigma_L. An ideal root channel with this coefficient and a source-zero child retains that root dependence. This diagnostic is not an assertion that arbitrary native derivatives can be recovered by differentiating a law approximation.

**Valid single-center control.** Keeping `rho_L=alpha hat_omega^2`, taking `rho_C=-zeta alpha w_c^2/sigma_c^6`, and leaving D and the leaves at alpha also works. Its largest source radius is `Lambda alpha tau^-2` and its dominant caller budget is `Lambda alpha^2 tau^-3`. It requires tau>=alpha^(1/3) for an O(alpha) first budget. Balancing both centers improves these guards without changing the coefficient/current clock powers.

The source note's reusable local first rule is also valid in its stated restricted setting: for a fixed legal opening with k>=1 C3 copies of this same old center and coefficient weight `omega^k/sigma_c^(3k)`, put one z_c on every center and `hat_omega^k` at a marked C0 root. Along a caller path containing m>=1 centers, the own-center row is at most `Lambda alpha^(m+1) tau^(-(m+1))`, hence O(alpha) for tau>=sqrt(alpha). Other ordinary ancestors improve it; the root's own leaf clock remains integrable. This requires bounded retained old injection rows, no hidden coefficient normalization beyond the declared fixed-order factors, and separately charged derivative-hit source types. It is a radius/first rule only.

## 5. The restricted one-Hilbert proof really does apply here

Partition **each occurrence** of the four-force spine into L–C and D–M. Each initial chunk retains exactly one physical slot, one G slot, one H slot, and its middle/side boundaries. The open chunk's proper cuts are bounded by the native normalized C0/C3 tree-cut certificate.

A G pairing or H pairing cannot self-contract inside an initial chunk because it has only one slot of that color. Middle and side contractions also join distinct chunks. One-side and both-side cap leaves retain their own physical slots. Hence every connected conditional family and every auxiliary Wick graph obtained from it has no initial self-loop and every initial block keeps a physical slot.

Merge connected groups, contracting **all** shared edges simultaneously. Operator Cauchy–Schwarz uses proper flattenings in both groups because both retain physical slots. It gives the product proper-cut bound. Isolating one final D-dimensional physical index gives one full-HS factor sqrt(D). Starting at a marked HS-energy chunk and using HS-times-operator multiplication propagates one actual Hilbert energy, including integration over shared old roots when the other factor bounds are uniform.

For auxiliary cumulants, only pairings connected across their argument groups survive. Each argument was already a connected force graph, so the final graph is connected; disconnected moment products are cancelled by the exact cumulant formula before assigning a connected norm bound.

There is also a transparent leading-energy check. Let T be the open six-force tensor with four physical slots and two G plus two H slots, separately symmetrized in the two auxiliary pairs. Its polynomial T(G,G,H,H) has four orthogonal chaos sectors. Thus

    E ||T(G,G,H,H)||_HS^2
      = ||Tr_G Tr_H T||_HS^2
        + 2 ||Tr_H T||_HS^2
        + 2 ||Tr_G T||_HS^2
        + 4 ||T||_HS^2
      <= Lambda D.

In the middle two norms, the surviving auxiliary pair remains a free tensor pair. Each of these partial traces is justified by the **marked chunk geometry**, not by a generic rule that tensor traces preserve proper cuts.

The latter generic rule is false: `T_(i,a,b,c,d)=e_i delta_ab delta_cd/D` has every proper cut at most one, but its two self-traces give the vector D e. Our checker verifies this control. Consuming a chunk's last physical mark or introducing a same-color self-trace requires a new certificate.

## 6. Clock/effective grades and the old-bank boundary

The balanced amplitudes do not change t. A k-spine-copy coefficient carries `omega^(2k)/sigma_c^(6k)`, whose center endpoint sum costs `Lambda tau^(-2k)`. In particular,

    intended main:               alpha^6 tau^-2,
    first conditional ring:      alpha^8 tau^-4,
    first pure auxiliary mix:    alpha^12 tau^-4.

At tau=alpha^(1/3), these are alpha^(16/3), alpha^(20/3), and alpha^(32/3). Thus the first returned conditional/auxiliary errors have a genuine effective-grade improvement over this input main. This is a bound for the displayed operation, not a force-only induction invariant.

The old eight-force one-cycle operation had positive endpoint slack. This operation does not inherit it. For h whole-old-bank connected factors containing a total of k root-spine copies, a conservative expansion of h-1 Riesz bridges has up to `2(h-1)` additional original heat derivatives. Assigning them to the center yields the explicit ledger

    tau^[-2k-2(h-1)],

with another tau^-1 if a separately requested caller score is used. For h main factors (k=h), the conservative current scale is `alpha^(6h) tau^[-4h+2]`; h=2 gives alpha^12 tau^-6. These are width allowances, not lower bounds and not native source constructors.

The exact exported symbol remains the finite connected expansion on the complete same q/G/H/readout ancestry, followed by the actual tilted shared public bank when appropriate. Pointwise cancellation of the old main cancels its own tilted descendants; new eight-and-higher families have new tilt descendants. Shared rotated q_+,q_- covariance widths, observer read sets, score factors, mark consumption, and cross-node bank correlations must all be retained. Independent-node logarithms may be added only after verifying the complete banks are independent in the relevant tilted reference and that no integrated public remains a retained observer.

A fixed-tau analytic all-cut/derivative allowance does not by itself supply an original-VALUE positive gradient producer for a grouped covariance/tilt coefficient. No general terminal gradient/curl, observer-preserving law, or all-order queue closure follows here.

## 8. A concrete next-certificate failure, with reroot caveat

Take the first eight-force trace offspring. Its four C3 centers are a=C1,b=D1,c=C2,d=D2, each initially attached to its own retained physical leaf. The middle edges are ab,cd and the side Wick edges are ac,bd. Choose the legitimate auxiliary pairings

    G: (ab,cd),
    H: (ac,bd).

Every edge of the center four-cycle a–b–d–c–a is now doubled. The complete graph has eight forces, four marks, and cycle number five. This is an actual positive auxiliary Wick contribution, not an arbitrary invented tensor network.

Any spanning tree must keep all four pendant leaf edges. Its center tree selects three different simple edges of C4, with one of the two parallel copies of each, so it is P4. There are `4*2^3=32` such spanning trees. Every retained center edge has a parallel cut twin. If that twin is closed by its own auxiliary color, any connected chunk containing both endpoint centers has two occurrences of that color and violates the sufficient no-initial-self-trace condition. Therefore every admissible marked chunk in a zero-side distinguished-spine term contains at most one center.

A spine through three or four centers has only its two endpoint physical marks after its side crosses are omitted by the intrinsic trace feedback. It cannot be covered by connected chunks that each retain a mark and contain at most one occurrence of every cut color. In particular, **every longest root-to-physical-leaf spine fails**, for all 32 spanning trees and all four choices of root leaf. Rooting at an endpoint center gives a four-center longest spine; rooting at an interior center gives a three-center longest spine.

Do not strengthen this to failure for every possible distinguished spine. Across all 384 directed distinct physical-leaf paths of these trees, 192 adjacent-center paths have only two centers and pass this particular chunk test; 192 three/four-center paths fail.

The short paths have a different, exact problem for the proposed recursive rule. Using one normalization `alpha z_c` at each center and keeping unrelated weights at the root, the eight-force input has weight `alpha^8 hat_omega^4 z_c^4`. A short root spine has two centers and two marked leaves, hence four forces; its zero-side variance has eight forces and weight `alpha^8 hat_omega^8 z_c^4`. Both have certified alpha/center-width grade `8-4 gamma` at tau=alpha^gamma. The additional unrelated-clock factor supplies no uniform positive alpha power. With ell centers on a longer path, that variance has `2ell+4` forces, center factor `z_c^(2ell)`, and grade increase `(2ell-4)(1-gamma)` over the input. For ell=3,4 this is positive when gamma<1, but those paths fail the sufficient marked-chunk test.

Thus this particular reopening/allocation rule does not inherit both its mark-preserving chunk certificate and a strict force/effective-grade gain: short paths preserve the former but do not certify the latter; long paths gain grade but lose the former. This is not a proof against a same-grade coefficient inversion, a different amplitude allocation, a new block certificate, or a different positive compiler. A short-spine/rerooted program would still need a complete analysis of its side-subtree and further descendants.
