## Result first

The affine-feedback source E0 has a two-original-VALUE, genuine full-gradient lift whose WHOLE energy is bounded by the actual original E energy. No unmarked companion is hidden in that lift. A known Gaussian rotation applied to E0 alone, while the old roots and nonlinear opposite baseline remain fixed, creates actual V input columns. On this localized fixture its VALUE change has a positive energy gain even at a fixed rotation angle. This is stronger than a common reparameterization, which indeed cannot change any complete covariance field.

The exact full-gradient lift supplies a positive one-block-pair skew selector for the complete physical orientation, including its feedback-Z term, at every retained finite clock root. A paired plus/minus rotated source supplies an additional explicitly E-marked full-gradient partner. Its mixed field selects the missing true Gram on the zero-auxiliary-root plane, but has explicitly listed additional blocks elsewhere. Its plane identity is not a full retained-root endpoint identity.

All of these are finite original-gradient VALUE programs. They are genuine positive progress beyond the earlier parity obstruction. They do not, by themselves, prove the old nonlinear baseline's joint endpoint law: the exact pair theorem retains its declared coarse roots and fresh passive p, not arbitrary fine records read by an old baseline. A baseline depending only on actually retained roots can be appended exactly; a baseline sharing integrated private/source records requires its additional conditional mean and cross-current contract.

    G(W)=κ[C0* g(C0W)−C1* g(C1W)]                    (2)

The source is anchored, so this also bounds its centered energy up to the usual numerical moment factor. It is not merely a bound for the selected physical row.

    D_Z E0=κq T A1 T >=κq m λ_min(T)² I.

The independent conditional-copy variance argument therefore gives

    e2(E0)>=κq m λ_min(T)² sqrt(d).

## 3. An actual rotated source, with the old baseline left fixed

Introduce one fresh standard V and fix an angle θ. Define

    U_θ=cosθ U+sinθ V,
    C0,θ=[cI,s cosθ I,0,s sinθ I],
    C1,θ=C0,θ−qT P_Z,
    Eθ=κT[g(C0,θ W')−g(C1,θ W')], W'=(S,U,Z,V).

The same formulas (2),(4),(5),(7) give a complete genuine-gradient lift Gθ with the SAME coisometry B based on its S block. Eθ now has actual V columns relative to the old owned (S,U,Z) and original auxiliary V. We have changed a VALUE source; we have not rotated the old baseline or its root identity outputs.

This distinction matters. Applying one common orthogonal map R to both derivative factors gives

    (J_E R)(J_N R)*=J_E J_N*.

It merely moves the old parity cancellation between coordinate blocks. No escape is claimed from that operation.

For this particular localized fixture, a fixed-angle change of E0 is nevertheless energy-small. Exactly,

    Eθ=κqT³Z+κδq T e1
          [ψ((C0,θ W')1/q)−ψ((C1,θ W')1/q)].         (8)

The affine Gaussian term does not depend on θ. Each remaining term is bounded by Cκq and supported on an O(q)-probability Gaussian slab. The scalar query variances are uniformly bounded below. Consequently, for every fixed finite p,

    ||Eθ−E0||p<=C_p κ q^(1+1/p)

This is an actual source coupling with a positive fixed grade. The generic first-based estimate O(κ|θ|sqrt(d)) is valid but needlessly weak here. It would not justify (9) for a general nonlocalized source.

differ only in the final component. Thus (9) remains valid with ALL original roots and the baseline retained exactly, under any fixed incoming Lipschitz in that final component. ## 5. A finite positive skew-selector architecture for Eθ

## 7. What is and is not retained about the unmarked baseline

The exact source approximation (9) keeps the original nonlinear N_h and every original root identically. It is therefore a valid joint VALUE re-entry, before any pair theorem is applied. The same is true of the exact identity G+−G−=G_nl+C_G if BOTH outputs are kept.

For the known C_G=L_G W', joint covariance blocks are explicit:

    Cov(C_G,W')=L_G, Cov(C_G)=L_G L_G*.

Its cross with a nonlinear source is not obtained by declaring independence. At a node with X standard and a source F(c_tR+v_tX), conditional Gaussian integration by parts gives

    Cov(F(c_tR+v_tX),C_G(c_tR+v_tX)|R)
       =v_t² E[D F(c_tR+v_tX)|R] L_G*.

This is an actual current. L_G is known, symmetric and O(κq), so an admitted known-linear gradient adapter can service such a current with its true small factor; none of these blocks can simply be discarded while claiming the old full joint output is unchanged.

For the nonlinear opposite baseline, its unmarked body is still present. In particular its second output is globally κ(1/20,1/2)V in the normalized midpoint convention. It does not acquire an E mark by pairing it with (18). The baseline's literal source has the six original VALUES from feedback, ancestors and its two final queries; its full-root first retains the O(κ/h) row.

The section 5 pair construction can retain N_h exactly IF its actual arguments are all functions of the R,p that the pair theorem retains. This is a straightforward deterministic append to the SAME-root coupling. It cannot do so merely because someone calls an integrated source-fine record an 'old root'. If N_h reads fine records that the pair comparison integrates, or aliases pair-private records, the required joint comparison must additionally give its conditional mean, self covariance, cross covariances with both pair outputs, and their incoming/caller currents. Neither the selected-word identity nor the small energy of (18) supplies those obligations.

The explicit nonzero field (12) demonstrates why an independent-baseline append would generally be wrong after the changed-source rotation. The full field (20) demonstrates a separate problem with extending the zero-plane Gram identity to arbitrary retained roots. These are precise failures of those proposed shortcuts, not a parity obstruction to V-dependent sources and not a theorem against all joint compilers.

## 8. Numerical floors, aliases and work

An original g VALUE error η_i in (2) contributes at most κ sum_i ||Ci||η_i to G and its actual B readout. The rotated and difference versions use the same literal maps. The chart h_R adds its real 1/(κL0v_t); the pair adds ρ; the output adds b or d. The known C_G subtraction has its own matrix-rounding floor. All original source, pair, fill, clock and known-row errors are absolute additive floors propagated through those displayed factors. None is absorbed by an unknown or zero actual e.

Known orthogonal rotations and fixed coisometric fills have bounded numerical norms. They do not generate empirical copies or divide by q. A small-angle use of (21) would divide by sin²θ and must be priced; the fixed-angle θ=π/4 used here avoids that bill. The slab energy gain depends on fixed finite p; it is not uniform as p tends to infinity.

Eθ alone costs two original VALUES; G_nl costs four. The old nonlinear midpoint baseline costs six. A generic joint Eθ/N_h evaluation therefore costs at most eight, before exact aliases. Adding the original six-query E shares its first four with the midpoint baseline and costs two additional old terminal calls, for ten generic queries total. Changed rotated queries must not be identified with original queries merely because their marginal Gaussian laws agree. All first/adjoint replays and dense known matrix arithmetic are also charged.
