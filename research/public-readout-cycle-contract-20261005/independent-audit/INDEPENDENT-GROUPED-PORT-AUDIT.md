## 7. Independent derivation of both obstructions

### 7.1 Retained public

For Q=epsilon cos(B)P+sqrt(1−epsilon^2 cos^2 B)Z,

    E[W | B,P] = aP+c epsilon cos(B)P.

If T is fresh and conditionally independent of (P,Z,G) given B, then E[T|B,P]=m(B). The corresponding baseline has mean aP+m(B). Jensen under any coupling retaining B,P gives the squared bound c^2 epsilon^2 E[cos^2(B)P^2]. Independence of B,P reduces it to c^2 epsilon^2(1+exp(−2))/2. For the raw source replace a by a+c m0 on both sides; the difference is unchanged.

The same argument survives an o(epsilon) finite native comparison by the triangle inequality. A correction depending explicitly on the old P can change the mean, so this does not prove impossibility of every joint producer. It proves that fresh independent packets or reparameterization alone cannot erase the old retained cross-channel current.

The ordinary joint-W2 corollary also passes. With F=cos(B)PW, the expectation gap is c epsilon E cos^2 B. The exact split is

    F−F' = (cos B−cos B')PW
             + cos B'(P−P')W + cos B'P'(W−W').

One Cauchy–Schwarz on these three terms gives the author's bracket sqrt(E[P^2W^2]+E[W^2]+E[(P')^2]). Every quantity in the bracket belongs to one marginal; taking the infimum over all couplings is therefore legitimate. The finite fourth-moment guard makes it O(1). This is not a claim that F is globally Lipschitz.

For Xepsilon=beta B+W, direct conditional Gaussian integration gives

    Phi_epsilon(theta)=exp(−v theta^2/2)
        E[exp(i beta theta B) exp(−ac epsilon theta^2 cos B)].

The Gaussian Fourier identity, independently derived by writing cos as two exponentials, is

    E exp(iuB) cos B = exp(−(u^2+1)/2) cosh u.
