# An executable observer block, and the exact native boundary

5 October 2026. Task C of the sealed repair-graph algebra. A scalar construction with exact retained-test identities, two finite-VALUE repairs, and a deliberately limited extension statement. No external publication. No trajectory iteration is used.

## 1. Result and scope

For the required force

    g(x)=c x+epsilon sin x,   0<epsilon<c,   c+epsilon<=1,
    W_alpha=B+alpha g(B),   B standard normal,   alpha>=0,

its entire zero-reserve observer family can be retained as one transport-functional generator. In one dimension its leading rank-one interaction has an exact, computable, finite-original-VALUE inverse. An explicit three-VALUE program, including the original call making W, gives two successive repairs with uniform retained-carrier errors O(alpha^3) and O(alpha^4). It has a positive probability law, a positive globally bounded scalar first, zero scalar curl, and no native-to-ideal floor.

This is an **explicit added carrier-aware transport port** in the raw original-VALUE language. It is not an application of the existing carrier-stable root-seed port: that port requires its new carrier H to be independent of the coefficient bank E. Here the carrier and coefficient caller are the same B, throughout. In the strict native marked-tree dictionary, this source remains outside its stated type rules. The construction supplies the missing scalar port rather than pretending it was already included.

The extension covers a finite-VALUE class of bounded rank-one velocities and this complete scalar stress family. It does not establish an inverse for every native higher-rank current, a general C2 finite-VALUE derivative oracle, a dimension-free all-rank native theorem, or an arbitrary-order unsmoothed theorem for a general force. Section 5 does provide a finite-order functional solver for this special sine family.

## 2. The tower is one functional current

For any bounded retained test eta(B), independent untouched keep Z if desired, and smooth compactly supported physical test phi, put

    X_alpha=B+alpha g(B)+sigma Z,   sigma>=0.

Then the exact path current is

    d/dalpha E[eta(B) phi(X_alpha)]
      = E[eta(B) g(B) phi'(X_alpha)].                     (2.1)

There is one coefficient and one derivative of the test. The infinitely many physical cumulants arise only upon expanding this same functional against exponential tests and then converting moments to cumulants. At alpha=0 and sigma=0, Gaussian tilting gives

    d/dalpha log E exp(theta W_alpha)|_(alpha=0)
      = theta E g(B+theta)
      = c theta^2+epsilon exp(-1/2) theta sin theta.       (2.2)

Thus the sine contribution to cumulant 2m is

    alpha epsilon exp(-1/2) (-1)^(m-1) (2m),  m>=1.

Equation (2.1), rather than this rank list, is the generator record. It is mass-zero, exact after every retained test eta, and uses the literal original VALUE g(B). No conditional independence between g(B) and B is asserted. Adding the same independent keep on both sides preserves all identities and bounds below; none of its coordinates is queried.

One must not use Gaussian integration by parts to replace arbitrary higher-rank currents at this retained boundary without retaining derivatives of eta. For example,

    E[eta C phi''(B)]
      = E[(B eta C-(eta C)') phi'(B)]

contains -eta' C. There is no universal retained-B relation that replaces C phi'' by a rank-one coefficient independent of eta when C is nonzero. Localizing eta and prescribing the first two derivatives of phi proves this pointwise. The construction below never makes that replacement.

## 3. Normalize the linear part honestly

Set

    s=1+alpha c,   beta=alpha epsilon/s,
    p(x)=(g(x)-c x)/epsilon=sin x,
    q(x)=(g(x+pi/2)-c(x+pi/2))/epsilon=cos x,
    F_beta(x)=x+beta p(x),   A_beta(x)=1+beta q(x).

Then W_alpha/s=F_beta(B) exactly. The scaling is part of the executed program, not an omitted covariance branch. Every comparison to N(0,1) below includes it. We work with 0<=beta<=1/8; alpha<=1/8 suffices. Write a=1-beta. Then

    a<=F_beta'(x)<=1+beta.

The inverse F_beta^(-1) exists as a monotone map, but is not called as an oracle. Both p and q are exact identities using original force VALUES at explicit callers. They exploit this specified sine family. Constants c and epsilon are known parameters; their precision is part of the numerical ledger if approximate.

## 4. Exact leading block at a fixed endpoint

Freeze beta and the endpoint X=F_beta(B)+sigma Z. Retain B and any declared external coefficient tapes E. Let v(B,E) be a finite-original-VALUE scalar program with deterministic bounds |v|<=V0 and |partial_B v|<=V1; include other tape firsts in its own ledger. If E contains caller values depending on B, V1 is the complete B derivative through those callers, not a derivative that freezes their ancestry. Define

    h_v(B,E)=v(B,E)/A_beta(B),
    Y_t=F_beta(B+t h_v(B,E))+sigma Z.

For every eta(B,E), the leading identity is at exactly the same endpoint X:

    d/dt E[eta phi(Y_t)]|_(t=0)
      = E[eta A_beta(B) h_v(B,E) phi'(X)]
      = E[eta v(B,E) phi'(X)].                          (4.1)

Hence the block L_beta:h -> A_beta h has the explicit right inverse h_beta:v -> v/A_beta. Its equality is conditional/retained, not merely after averaging B. For the stated class, the right inverse is itself a finite original-VALUE program: one call at B+pi/2 supplies q, plus the actual calls computing v. Division is safe since A_beta>=a. No selected-mean, conditional-expectation, derivative, tensor, or law oracle is invoked.

The complete carrier first is controlled directly:

    |h_v|<=V0/a,
    |partial_B h_v|<=V1/a+beta V0/a^2=:H1,
    partial_B Y_t=A_beta(B+t h_v)(1+t partial_B h_v).

For |t|H1<1 the carrier map is increasing, globally Lipschitz, and is a genuine scalar gradient. Other retained/tape firsts obey the literal chain rule; for example partial_E Y_t=t A_beta(B+t h_v) partial_E h_v. The keep first is sigma and the keep is never read. Gaussian L2 bounds follow from these explicit first/size bounds. Positivity is the actual pushforward of the Gaussian inputs, not a signed density.

The zero-reserve observer interaction is now inside L_beta. The nonlinearity left over has an exact integral identity:

    Y_t-X-t v
      = beta t^2 h_v^2 integral_0^1 (1-u) p''(B+u t h_v) du.  (4.2)

Its bound is beta t^2 V0^2/(2a^2). Thus the remaining displacement has an additional t grade, without assigning positive reserve to B. If beta is also small it carries another beta factor. This is a normed functional block statement; it does not require rank-local finiteness of (2.2).

Unbounded v require their actual bounds. In particular v=-g(B) cannot be inserted into the displayed *global* first theorem merely using Gaussian moments: its h_v' can be unbounded. Separating the linear part first is substantive. The bounded sine v used next meets the hypotheses.

## 5. A non-iterative, two-stage, finite-VALUE inverse

For any scalar input x define

    h(x)=-p(x)/A_beta(x),
    k(x)=beta p(x)^3/[2 A_beta(x)^3],
    Q1(x)=x+beta h(x),
    Q2(x)=x+beta h(x)+beta^2 k(x).

These are two terms of a fixed algebraic source repair. There is no trajectory or inverse fixed-point loop. Both stages use the same p(x),q(x) cache. In particular Q2 is not a symbolic instruction to evaluate F_beta^(-1).

Let

    H=1/a,   K=beta/(2a^3),
    C2(beta)=H K+beta K^2/2+(H+beta K)^3/6.

The exact identities A h=-p and A k=-beta p'' h^2/2 hold because p''=-p. Put delta1=beta h and delta2=beta h+beta^2 k. Taylor's theorem with its integral remainder gives

    e1(x):=F_beta(Q1(x))-x
      = beta delta1^2 integral_0^1 (1-u) p''(x+u delta1) du,
    |e1(x)|<=beta^3 H^2/2.                              (5.1)

For the second stage the whole beta^3 local curvature term cancels, leaving the exact expression

    e2(x):=F_beta(Q2(x))-x
      = beta^4 p''(x) h(x) k(x)
        +beta^5 p''(x) k(x)^2/2
        +beta delta2^3/2 integral_0^1 (1-u)^2 p'''(x+u delta2) du,
    |e2(x)|<=beta^4 C2(beta).                           (5.2)

The last integral's factor is 1/2, so its bound is |beta delta2^3|/6. These are identities for the executed finite programs, not a native error estimate being differentiated.

Execute the actual repaired samples

    x=W_alpha/s=F_beta(B),
    Z1=Q1(x),   Z2=Q2(x).

Since F_beta has lower derivative a, (5.1)-(5.2) give the **uniform, carrierwise** bounds

    |Z1-B|<=beta^3/(2a^3),
    |Z2-B|<=beta^4 C2(beta)/a.                           (5.3)

Consequently the retained-(B,E) coupling costs and unconditional W2 costs obey the same bounds. The same untouched keep sigma Z can be appended, with no extra cost or sharing. The leading defects, uniformly on compact x or B sets, are

    Z1-B=-(beta^3/2) sin(B)^3+O(beta^4),
    Z2-B=(beta^4/6) sin(B)^3 cos(B)+O(beta^5).             (5.4)

Thus neither claimed gain is a vacuous bound: there are nonzero third- and fourth-order errors. Since beta=alpha epsilon/(1+alpha c) with fixed epsilon>0, the stages gain from alpha to alpha^3 and then alpha^4. No cumulant-rank cutoff was used.

### Exact current/test pairing for both stages

Put d_j(B)=Z_j-B. For every bounded retained eta and smooth compactly supported phi,

    E[eta(phi(Z_j+sigma Z)-phi(B+sigma Z))]
      = integral_0^1 E[eta d_j phi'(B+u d_j+sigma Z)] du.  (5.5)

This is an exact mass-zero probability-path current, with literal named tapes and the same keep. It proves the weak residual bound with ||eta||infinity ||phi'||infinity times (5.3). Its endpoints vary along the explicitly displayed probability path; no moving-endpoint expression is falsely identified with a finite jet at B.

For an exact finite jet at the fixed endpoint X0=B+sigma Z, one may instead write

    E[eta(phi(X0+d_j)-phi(X0))]
      = E[eta d_j phi'(X0)]
        +E[eta d_j^2 integral_0^1(1-u) phi''(X0+u d_j) du]. (5.6)

The second term is an explicit remainder, not a dropped rank-two current. For Q2 its norm has grade at least eight in beta under a bounded phi''. An arbitrary finite higher Taylor order has its ordinary integral remainder. Equations (4.1), (5.5), and (5.6) separate the fixed-endpoint leading block, the exact all-test current, and finite-jet bookkeeping.

### Finite-order scalar meta-generator

The same special family admits an explicit finite rule beyond the two worked stages. Freeze beta and introduce a separate formal scalar t. Set h_1=-p/A. For n>=2 set

    h_n(x;beta)=-(beta/A(x)) sum_(r=2)^n [p^(r)(x)/r!]
                   sum_(n_1+...+n_r=n; n_i>=1) product_i h_(n_i)(x;beta). (5.7)

Every h on the right has index at most n-1. This is a triangular finite calculation. It cancels, successively, the t^n coefficients in

    F_beta(x+sum_(j=1)^N t^j h_j)-(F_beta(x)-t p(x)).

The first two h_j are exactly h and k above. Every derivative p^(r) is one of p,q,-p,-q, so the complete finite list h_1,...,h_N is computed from the same two original VALUES at x and x+pi/2. No additional derivative query or exact inverse query appears.

For each fixed N and any fixed b0<1, the h_j and their x derivatives have computable uniform bounds on beta in [0,b0] and x modulo 2pi. One obtains these directly from (5.7), a lower bound A>=1-b0, the product/quotient rules, and |p^(r)|<=1. All h_j with j>=2 have an explicit beta factor and bounded remaining factor. Choose a computable b_N<=b0 so that

    sum_(j=1)^N b_N^j sup_(beta<=b0,x) |partial_x h_j| <= 1/2.

This is an actual first guard, rather than a convergence assumption. Then Q_N(x)=x+sum_(j=1)^N beta^j h_j is globally increasing with first between 1/2 and 3/2 for beta<=b_N. The finite Taylor formula and (5.7) yield a computable bound

    |F_beta(Q_N(x))-x| <= C_N beta^(N+2),
    |Q_N(F_beta(B))-B| <= C_N beta^(N+2)/(1-beta).        (5.8)

For an explicit finite formula, let M_j bound |h_j| on beta<=b0. With every j_i in {1,...,N}, a valid constant is

    C_N = sum_(r=2)^N (1/r!) sum_(j_1+...+j_r>N)
                 b0^(j_1+...+j_r-N-1) product_i M_(j_i)
          +(sum_(j=1)^N b0^(j-1) M_j)^(N+1)/(N+1)!.

The first sum bounds the uncanceled powers of delta above degree N. The second term bounds the order-(N+1) Taylor remainder of p. The factor beta multiplying p supplies the displayed extra grade. The accompanying script computes the M_j and derivative majorants by finite product/quotient rules and records exact rational b_N and C_N for N=1,...,6 in finite-order-budgets.json; decimal fields are only display approximations. The coefficients are periodic bounded rational functions of p,q and beta, so this gives a finite original-VALUE realization at every requested fixed order, with an explicit order-dependent guard and precision bill. Equations (5.5)-(5.6) apply unchanged to its literal displacement d_N.

A direct composition enumeration in (5.7) uses at most O(N^2 2^N) scalar operations and exactly the same three original VALUES including W; cached polynomial arithmetic can improve that coarse bound. The operation count, the first guard b_N, C_N, and numerical sensitivity may grow with N. No bound uniform in N, favorable bit complexity, generic-force oracle reduction, or native all-rank closure is inferred. This is a special-family finite functional solver that avoids the rank tower, not merely a symbolic generating-function inverse.

## 6. Firsts, curl, ownership, positivity, and cost

The exact derivatives are

    h'(x)=-(q(x)+beta)/A_beta(x)^2,
    k'(x)=3 beta p(x)^2(q(x)+beta)/(2 A_beta(x)^4).

Use

    H1=(1+beta)/a^2,
    K1=3 beta(1+beta)/(2a^4),
    rho=beta H1+beta^2 K1.

For beta<=1/8, rho<0.191. Hence

    1-rho<=Q2'(x)<=1+rho,
    (1-beta)(1-rho)<=dZ2/dB<=(1+beta)(1+rho).

Q1 has the analogous stronger bound without beta^2 K1. Thus all complete firsts are globally controlled, the maps are increasing diffeomorphisms of the line, and their output densities are positive by ordinary change of variables. Scalar curl is identically zero. The first bounds are derived from the actual finite program, never from a law-distance remainder. Without a keep, the density formula is the standard Gaussian density evaluated at the inverse divided by the positive Jacobian; it is a proof identity and no inverse is executed. With a keep, convolution is positive as well.

Ownership ledger:

- B is simultaneously the original carrier and coefficient caller. It stays readable and is retained in every identity.
- Original W uses one VALUE at B. The normalization x=W/s is literal.
- The repair calls g(x) and g(x+pi/2). These callers depend on B through the old program; their entire ancestry and firsts are retained. They are not renamed independent banks. The program queries the original g at a new caller; it never declares the completed output x or Qj to be a new original gradient primitive.
- Only scalar arithmetic, two shifted force VALUES, and the already executed old VALUE occur. No new Gaussian carrier, root-seed innovation, expectation, derivative oracle, or conditional-law sampler is inserted.
- The optional final keep is original, independent, untouched, and never queried. No per-stage keep is added.
- The old VALUE is replayed if its source/caller version changes. Exact caching requires the identical x and original force version.

Cost: one VALUE constructs W; two additional VALUES produce both Q1 and Q2 from their shared cache, for **three original VALUES total**. Q2 adds O(1) arithmetic over Q1. Evaluating F_beta(Qj) solely for a diagnostic costs an additional VALUE per diagnostic and is not in the sampler. Computing proof firsts does not add a sampling oracle call; if an actual first/HVP sweep is requested, charge the three original call sites and their complete caller paths in the existing first-action model. The mathematical sampling bill has zero inverse-alpha VALUE exponent at these fixed two orders. It gives no statement about arbitrary-order growth.

All formulas above use exact original VALUES, as does the original source language. With approximate force calls of uniform absolute error delta_g<=epsilon/8, fixed exact parameters, and beta<=1/8, elementary differentiation of Q2 as a rational function of p and q bounds the additional output error by at most 5 alpha delta_g, before separate arithmetic/parameter floors. The denominator remains positive. This conservative bound includes propagation of the old W error and both new force errors. Caller rounding propagates by the displayed global firsts; parameter errors and cancellation in g(x)-cx must have their actual interval/precision bill. The global first and curl certificates above concern exact VALUES (or a separately certified smooth VALUE implementation); a uniform value-error bound alone does not control derivatives of an arbitrary noisy implementation. This is not a free exact-real implementation claim.

## 7. Higher-rank interaction and the original dictionary

There is also a precise finite-rank algebraic block. If an admitted current acts on a test pulled through F_beta, the ordinary Bell-polynomial chain rule gives

    D_b^r[phi(F_beta(b))]
      = sum_(k=1)^r Bell_(r,k)(F_beta',...,F_beta^(r-k+1)) phi^(k)(F_beta(b)).

For a finite coefficient array C_0,...,C_m, this is a triangular change of current coefficients with diagonal 1,A,A^2,...,A^m. It is invertible by backward substitution, without integrating over B or differentiating retained eta. All derivatives of F_beta in this specified sine family use the same p,q cache and known signs; Bell coefficients are computed by a finite recurrence. Thus this is a computable exact **coefficient block** at any fixed rank. It is not a positive source theorem for all of its inverse coefficients.

In particular, the inverse of even a constant output rank-one coefficient is 1/A_beta(B), which reads the live carrier. Such coefficients fail the existing root-seed requirement that its coefficient bank be independent of the new source-zero H. Identifying H=B would violate that requirement. Keeping a new independent H would instead change the endpoint and would not represent this block. This is a type-level nonmembership in the stated native carrier-stable subdictionary, not a semantic no-go theorem for all VALUE programs.

Section 4 supplies an actual new rank-one source for the bounded finite-VALUE class. For an arbitrary high-rank inverse Bell coefficient, the exact missing item is a **carrier-correlated native-current realization port**, with its complete caller first, terminal curl, retained-current identity, positive sampler, and cost. Neither the Bell inverse nor the root-seed theorem supplies it. If a native packet is used, its existing quadratic native-to-ideal floor must remain explicit at and above that floor; this construction never upgrades that theorem into an exact native jet.

A further sharp retained-boundary warning: one cannot demand a positive conditional law with only the first-order signed current and no second-order displacement moment. Conditional on B, a nonzero deterministic first displacement v requires a nonzero order-two moment. Thus the appropriate repair target is a genuine transport/probability path, with its required phi'' term, or a complete declared current. The derivation in Section 4 compares to X+t v, whose own quadratic test term is retained; it does not attempt the impossible truncation to a signed first-order law.

## 8. Dimension and general-C2 limits

For the separable D-dimensional family g_j(x)=c_j x_j+epsilon_j sin x_j with known diagonal parameters, apply this construction coordinatewise. A single vector VALUE at the vector caller and another at caller+(pi/2)1 supply every sine and cosine. The total remains three vector VALUES. The Jacobian is diagonal and positive, so terminal curl is zero. The strong/W2 error is at most sqrt(D) times the largest coordinate bound in (5.3), or the Euclidean sum of the actual coordinate bounds. All original coordinatewise ownership remains explicit. A fixed orthogonal change of coordinates is allowed with its literal caller/readout transforms and known parameters.

For a general nonseparable gradient g, analytic invertibility of Id+alpha g under a lower Jacobian bound does not imply a finite-original-VALUE inverse source. Even the block (I+alpha Dg)^(-1) requires a derivative that the generic VALUE language does not provide exactly. Any finite adaptive VALUE algorithm can be defeated by a smooth compactly supported perturbation vanishing at all queried points but changing Dg at the target; a sufficiently small perturbation preserves strict monotonicity/convexity in the scalar case. Thus no universal exact finite-VALUE derivative-block oracle follows from a general C2 assumption. Finite differences can supply approximate blocks only with their actual modulus-of-continuity/error and first/curl bills.

Moreover, in D>1, composing gradient maps need not be a gradient map: products of their symmetric Hessians at different locations need not commute. An approximate finite inverse may have nonzero terminal curl even when its displacement is small. One must prove a new curl-preserving realization, pay an explicitly allowed curl bound, or restrict to the separable/commuting case above. The scalar proof alone does none of these for a general force.

If g is only C2 with no quantitative modulus beyond its stated bounds, the fourth-order scalar remainder used here is not automatically available. It used the explicit sine's bounded third derivative. A C2 analogue has its actual continuity-modulus remainder, and a merely C1 original gradient requires still different bounds. Finally, no fixed-order result here resolves the separate heat-trace escape or the unbounded-rank native source-image problem.
