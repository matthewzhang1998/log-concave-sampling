## 1. Exact retained current and leading inverse

For each fixed bounded retained test eta(B,E), differentiating the physical test along the displayed path yields

    d/dt E[eta phi(F_beta(B+t h)+sigma Z)] at t=0
      = E[eta A_beta(B) h phi'(F_beta(B)+sigma Z)].

The coefficient identity A_beta h=v is pointwise in the retained record. There is no averaging over B to conceal a correlation and no derivative of eta. The endpoint is the same on both sides. B can therefore remain simultaneously the coefficient caller and physical carrier in this new port.

The inverse h=v/A_beta is an exact finite VALUE expression for the declared bounded finite-VALUE velocity class: cosine is recovered from one shifted original force call, and A_beta is bounded below by a=1-beta. The derivative formula gives

    |h'| <= V1/a + beta V0/a^2.

Consequently |t| times this upper bound less than one makes the carrier map strictly increasing and globally Lipschitz. Bounded displacement makes it onto the line. Other tape derivatives must still have their own declared bounds, as the report says.

The exact nonlinear remainder is

    beta t^2 h^2 integral_0^1 (1-u) p''(B+u t h) du.

Its bound beta t^2 V0^2/(2a^2) is correct. The extra t factor is a genuine improvement over the linear current without assigning a reserve to B; with beta=O(alpha), there is an additional alpha factor. This is a rank-one transport statement, not an inference that all high-rank currents can be rewritten as rank one at the retained-B boundary.

The warning about integration by parts is necessary and correct. With arbitrary retained eta, the derivative of eta appears. A purported universal coefficient converting phi'' to phi' for all retained tests would imply a pointwise identity between independently prescribable derivatives of phi and thus fails for a nonzero coefficient.

## 2. Observer tower and linear normalization

Gaussian tilting gives exactly

    partial_alpha log E exp(theta W_alpha) at alpha=0
      = c theta^2 + epsilon exp(-1/2) theta sin(theta).

The displayed rank-2m sine coefficient is the first-order-in-alpha cumulant contribution, with coefficient epsilon exp(-1/2)(-1)^(m-1)(2m). It is not an exact finite-alpha formula for the cumulant. The surrounding derivative calculation makes that interpretation clear.

The normalization is executed: W_alpha/(1+alpha c)=F_beta(B). The linear covariance contribution is therefore not omitted. Both sine and cosine identities use the original force with known c and epsilon; no derivative oracle is hidden in the proof's trigonometric notation.

## 3. Two-stage algebraic repair

Independent substitution verifies

    A h=-p,  A k=-beta p'' h^2/2.

For delta1=beta h, the constant and linear terms in F_beta(x+delta1)-x cancel exactly, leaving the stated second-order integral remainder. For delta2=beta h+beta^2 k, the term beta^3 p'' h^2/2 cancels against beta^2 A k. The surviving terms are precisely

    beta^4 p'' h k + beta^5 p'' k^2/2
      + beta delta2^3/2 integral_0^1 (1-u)^2 p'''(x+u delta2) du.

The factor 1/2 preceding the last integral is correct, and its bound is |beta delta2^3|/6. Thus the stated C2(beta) bounds the entire residual. Applying the inverse Lipschitz bound 1/a to F_beta(Z_j)-F_beta(B) gives the uniform, carrierwise errors in (5.3), without executing an inverse.

The leading errors are also correct:

    Z1-B = -beta^3 sin(B)^3/2 + O(beta^4),
    Z2-B = beta^4 sin(B)^3 cos(B)/6 + O(beta^5).

These coefficients are nonzero. Since fixed epsilon>0 gives beta asymptotic to alpha epsilon, the alpha gains are genuinely third order and then fourth order. Neither stage is a trajectory iteration; both are algebraic outputs of one shared cache.

The exact all-test identity (5.5) is a probability-path current with an explicitly moving endpoint. Equation (5.6) separately gives the leading fixed-endpoint current and its retained second-order test remainder. These are not conflated. In particular the second-order displacement moment is kept, and for Q2 it is O(beta^8) under a bounded second derivative of the test.

## 3A. Finite-order scalar meta-generator

The extension (5.7) is a correct triangular recurrence. At fixed beta, use the separate formal parameter t and delta(t)=sum t^j h_j. Expanding the stated homotopy gives

    F_beta(x+delta)-(F_beta(x)-t p)
      = A delta+t p+beta sum_(r>=2) p^(r) delta^r/r!.

The coefficient of t is A h1+p, and that of t^n is exactly the left side canceled by (5.7). Every right-hand h index is less than n. At t=beta, F_beta(x)-t p=x. This proves the claimed source target without an inverse or trajectory oracle.

After Taylor expansion to order N, the remaining polynomial terms have t degree at least N+1 and still carry the outer beta. The Taylor remainder has the same beta prefactor and is bounded by beta |delta|^(N+1)/(N+1)!. Substitution t=beta therefore gives beta^(N+2), as claimed. The coefficients h_j depend on beta, but they are uniformly bounded on a fixed beta interval; their parameter dependence does not invalidate these formal coefficient cancellations.

For explicitness, if M_j bounds |h_j| on beta<=b0<1, one valid constant is

    C_N = sum_(r=2)^N (1/r!)
            sum_(j_1,...,j_r in {1,...,N}; sum j_i>N)
              b0^(sum j_i-N-1) product_i M_(j_i)
          + [sum_(j=1)^N b0^(j-1) M_j]^(N+1)/(N+1)!.

This is exactly the finite expansion implemented by the rational-budget routine. Bounds on h_j and their derivatives follow recursively from product and quotient rules. The script's initial derivative bound D1=1/(1-b0)^2 is valid: |q+beta|<=A for the sine/cosine pair, so |h1'|<=1/(1-beta), which is even stronger. Its recurrence for later D_j correctly includes both differentiation of the product sum and differentiation of A^(-1).

The chosen b_N=min(b0,1/[2 sum D_j]) enforces the stated derivative guard because b_N<1 implies sum b_N^j D_j<=b_N sum D_j<=1/2. Thus Q_N is strictly increasing with first in [1/2,3/2], and its complete composed carrier first lies in [(1-beta)/2,3(1+beta)/2]. The periodic bounded displacement makes it onto. The prior current identity, positivity argument, and scalar curl certificate apply unchanged.

All p derivatives are the same four-cycle p,q,-p,-q, so every fixed N still uses exactly the original three force VALUES. The concrete added source implementation was checked against the independent coefficient formula for N=1,...,6. Direct composition enumeration costs at most the claimed coarse O(N^2 2^N) scalar operations. The number of arithmetic operations, small-beta guard, error constants, and sensitivity can depend on N; no uniform-order convergence or favorable bit complexity follows.

The rational-budget calculations for N=1,...,6 give b_N=1/8 and first-guard sums between 0.1632653 and 0.1687996, all below 1/2. Their C_N values are approximately 0.6530612, 0.3636453, 0.2030045, 0.1206142, 0.0759751, and 0.0499359. The exact fractions are retained in the budget file.

As a separate numerical check, a 90-digit implementation generated h_j with truncated polynomial convolution instead of composition enumeration. At x=0.43 and beta halved from 1/1024 to 1/2048, the residual orders for N=1,...,6 were approximately 2.9985, 3.9985, 4.9909, 5.9924, 6.9591, and 7.9673, consistent with N+2. The exact finite Taylor proof, rather than these rates, establishes the bound. This check is reproducible with `python audit_check_high_precision.py` using mpmath; its output is saved in `audit-high-precision.json` and `audit-high-precision-run.txt`.

This extension establishes arbitrary requested fixed order for the special sine family only. It does not furnish a generic C2 derivative oracle, an all-rank native current realization, an N-uniform first/precision guard, or a general-force unsmoothed theorem.

## 4. Complete firsts, scalar curl, and positivity

Direct differentiation confirms

    rho = 909/4802 = 0.18929612661391088 < 0.191.

The positive rational upper bounds increase with beta on this interval, so the endpoint check bounds every beta in [0,1/8]. It follows that Q2' and the complete composed derivative Q2'(F_beta(B))F_beta'(B) are globally positive and bounded as claimed. Bounded corrections to the identity make both Qj onto, giving increasing diffeomorphisms and positive pushforward densities. The optional independent unchanged keep preserves positivity. ## 5. Literal execution, ownership, and precision

The sample program has exactly three original force calls: g(B), g(x), and g(x+pi/2). It computes x from the first call and uses the last two to share p,q between both repaired outputs. The recorded sample callers are approximately 0.73, 0.7496138127942263, and 2.320410139589123. No source zero is renamed independent, no new Gaussian is introduced, and no keep coordinate is queried. Diagnostic evaluations of F_beta(Qj) are not charged to the sampler, appropriately.

The precision claim can be independently bounded more tightly than stated. For force-value errors at most delta_g<=epsilon/8, perturbed p,q have magnitude at most P=9/8. Their denominator is at least

    1-beta P >= 55/64.

For R(x,p,q)=x-beta p/A+beta^3 p^3/(2A^3), independent interval derivative bounds at the worst allowed beta give

    |partial_p R|/beta <= 1.210375,
    |partial_q R|/beta <= 0.198062.

The old x error is at most alpha delta_g/s and propagates through the exact Q2 with first at most 1+rho. Since beta/epsilon=alpha/s, the total value error is at most

    (1+rho+1.210375+0.198062) alpha delta_g/s
      < 2.598 alpha delta_g.

This verifies the conservative stated bound 5 alpha delta_g, before arithmetic and parameter errors. It also verifies denominator safety without any differentiability assumption on the individual oracle errors.

**Precision scope caveat:** this is an output-value/coupling error bound. A uniform value-error bound alone does not control the derivatives or monotonicity of a noisy force implementation. The proved complete-first, curl, and positive-density certificates apply to the exact VALUE program. The report states its exact-VALUE premise; future summaries should retain this distinction. A noisy or rounded implementation remains a positive pushforward measure, but positive smooth density and exact first bounds require additional implementation-specific hypotheses.

## 6. Native dictionary and higher-rank limits

The Bell-polynomial coefficient transform is upper triangular in the displayed indexing, with diagonal 1,A,...,A^m. The backward substitution is correct and does not differentiate retained eta. It is a finite-rank coefficient identity, not a positive realization theorem for each inverse coefficient.

The existing root-seed port requires the new source-zero H to be independent of the coefficient bank E. Here even 1/A_beta(B) reads the same B used as the carrier. Identifying H=B violates that contract; replacing it by an independent H changes the retained endpoint. The manuscript expressly describes this as nonmembership in that stated typed subdictionary rather than a semantic impossibility of VALUE computation.

The new scalar raw-VALUE port is therefore real additional content, but not evidence that the old native theorem already contained it. The report properly leaves a carrier-correlated positive realization port for arbitrary inverse Bell coefficients unresolved and preserves the existing quadratic native-to-ideal floor whenever an actual native packet is used. There is no claim that a norm floor can be differentiated into an exact jet.

The generic C2 limitation is sound: finitely many force values do not determine the derivative at a target point, since a small smooth perturbation can vanish at all queried points while changing that derivative. In a strictly monotone scalar interior example the perturbation can preserve the required structural bounds. The argument excludes a universal exact finite-VALUE derivative-block oracle; it does not prohibit approximations with paid regularity and precision bounds. The fourth-order sine proof specifically uses a bounded third derivative and does not transfer unchanged to an arbitrary C2 force.
