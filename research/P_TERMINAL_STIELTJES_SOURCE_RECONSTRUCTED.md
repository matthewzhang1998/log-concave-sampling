## Exact source and terminal decomposition

P:R^n→R^d is coisometric. Let

    F(W)=(r/√a)∇V(q(W)),   f=P*F,
    q(W)=b+√a PW+Ψ(W),
    Lip(∇V)≤1,   Lip_W Ψ≤√a a^g,   0<g≤1.

Write κ0=r a^g. Then Lip f≤r(1+a^g) and its full recorded curl is at most2κ0.

For the actual finite signed-twin physical proxy, the terminal identity is

    Dp_t^{+,[K]}=H_t^{[K]}(Pbar+A_t Dp_N^{+,[K−1]}).

Thus its physical readout is one original terminal gradient at its actual plus query, with a θ-only stored zero-trajectory offset. Both original force and finite proxy use Pbar=[P,0_aux] on the common padded tape. The proxy readout B is not substituted for Pbar.

The canonical same-version centering is

    H_fin(W)=H_raw(W)−H_raw(0)+F_actual(0),
    E(W)=F_actual(W)−H_fin(W).

E is a difference of two terminal-gradient records plus a W-constant offset. Their Ψ records and internal paths remain distinct and actual. A W-constant offset has zero circulation. Use the sum of their κ0 bounds. Their individual energies do not inherit the small E mark; a later orientation producer must preserve an intact E occurrence.

This terminal premise must hold for the complete actual force of the chosen generation. A retained surrogate alone cannot replace it without its separately charged VALUE error.

## Continuous and finite identities

For a closed rectifiable loop γ, the chain rule gives

    ∮f(W)·dW=−(r/a)∮∇V(q(W))·dΨ(W).                 (1)

Indeed dq=√a P dW+dΨ, and the integral of dV(q) is zero. Lipschitz Ψ suffices; no D²Ψ or derivative of a Hessian is used.

Fix a translated polygon W_i=W+ℓ_i, ℓ_N=ℓ_0. The ℓ_i are fixed with respect to W. At each distinct node compute the actual state Ψ_i and terminal gradient g_i=∇V(q(W_i)), sharing the closing node identically. Emit

    C_N(W)=−r/(2a) Σ_i(g_i+g_{i+1})·(Ψ_{i+1}−Ψ_i). (2)

This is a finite scalar original-gradient/state VALUE program. For E subtract its two complete terminal constructions on the SAME polygon/master record. Replacing every g_i by g_i−g_0 is an exact algebraic identity because the state increments telescope.

Let L_q=√a(1+a^g), L_Ψ=√a a^g and L_poly=Σ|ℓ_{i+1}−ℓ_i|. Globally,

    |C_N|≤(r/a)L_q L_Ψ L_poly²,
    ||D_W C_N||≤2(r/a)L_q L_Ψ L_poly
               =2κ0(1+a^g)L_poly.                     (3)

The terms differentiating g cost (r/a)L_q L_Ψ L_poly. For terms differentiating Ψ, cyclic summation by parts pairs a single DΨ_i with (g_{i+1}−g_{i−1})/2. The total gradient increment is bounded by L_q L_poly, giving the second equal cost. There is no node-count factor and no assumed finite derivative convergence.

If the actual exterior paths satisfy |Dθq_i[h]|≤C_q|h| and |DθΨ_i[h]|≤C_Ψ|h|, then

    |DθC_N[h]|≤(r/a)(C_q L_Ψ+L_q C_Ψ)L_poly |h|.     (4)

A physical-center caller of order(r/√a)a^g L_poly requires BOTH C_q=O(1) and C_Ψ=O(a^g). The latter is not implied by Lip_W Ψ. Caller-dependent polygon shapes add their own paths.

## Actual quadrature bill

Let C(γ) be(1) over the same polygon. Under these C1 bounds alone,

    |C_N−C(γ)|≤r/(2a)L_q L_Ψ Σ|ℓ_{i+1}−ℓ_i|².       (5)

Compare the integral on each edge with its two endpoint Stieltjes sums, then average. Subdivision of every edge into M equal pieces divides this bound by M, while leaving(3)–(4) unchanged. HVP outputs are not differentiated.

Finite trapezoidal circulation is not exactly zero for every gradient source. For a sharp rule-level diagnostic, take n=d=1, P=1, V(q)=q²/2,

    Ψ_M(x)=√a a^g cos(π Mx)/(π M),

with even M. On polygon0→1→2→0, subdividing EACH edge into M equal pieces gives exactly

    C_N(0)=−2κ0/(π M),  while C(γ)=0.

The first two cosine trapezoidal sums vanish and the return edge samples cosines1; discrete integration by parts gives the formula. This is a limitation of this finite rule under C1 bounds, not a universal impossibility theorem.
