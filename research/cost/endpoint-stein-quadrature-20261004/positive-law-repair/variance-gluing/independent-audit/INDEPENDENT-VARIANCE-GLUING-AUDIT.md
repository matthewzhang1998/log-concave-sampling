## 1. Exact transition and endpoint conditioning

Write `s2=1-r^2` and `Delta=t^2-r^2`, where `0<=r<t<=1`. Conditional on X, the forward Markov construction gives

    E(Y_t|X)=tX,
    E(Y_r|X)=rX,
    Var(Y_t|X)=(1-t^2)I,
    Var(Y_r|X)=s2 I,
    Cov(Y_t,Y_r|X)=(r/t)(1-t^2)I.

Gaussian block conditioning therefore gives

    E(Y_t|X,Y_r=z)
      = [r(1-t^2)/(t s2)] z + [Delta/(t s2)] X,
    Var(Y_t|X,Y_r=z)
      = [(1-t^2)Delta/(t^2 s2)] I.

The residual covariance is independent of X and z. The residual can consequently be generated by a fresh independent Gaussian after drawing X from its true conditional law. Bayes' rule gives exactly the source's `nu_(r,z)`: the factor `exp(-|X|^2/2)` in mu and the `Y_r|X` likelihood complete the square to `|X-rz|^2/(2s2)`.

Thus (1) is the genuine same-path reverse conditional kernel. At r=0 it uses X~mu and produces the correct unconditional time-t marginal; at t=1 it reduces to X with zero additional Gaussian variance. There is no division by zero because r<t implies r<1 and t>0. Independent endpoint noises selected separately at times r and t would generally give a different joint law and are not used here.

The identity is analytical. It does not supply an implementable X~nu service or authorize a conditional-sampling oracle.

## 2. H, covariance, and the C2 boundary

For the strongly convex conditional potential

    V(x)=|x-rz|^2/(2s2)+U(x),

one integration by parts gives

    E X=rz-s2 E g,
    Cov(g,X)=s2[E Dg-Cov(g)],
    Cov(X)=s2 I-s2 Cov(X,g).

The matrices in the second line are symmetric. Consequently, for

    H=E Dg-Cov(g),

the source's identities follow:

    Cov(X)=s2 I-s2^2 H,
    D_z m=(r/s2)Cov(g,X)=rH.

The derivative of the expectation with respect to z is obtained by differentiating the Gaussian likelihood, not by differentiating Dg. These arguments use only U in C2, bounded continuous symmetric Dg, and Gaussian tails. They require no third derivative or Hessian-continuity rate.

For each vector u, the inverse-Hessian variance inequality applied to `u·g` gives

    u^T Cov(g) u
      <= E[u^T Dg (s2^(-1)I+Dg)^(-1) Dg u].

At each individual x the middle inverse and both copies of Dg are functions of the same symmetric matrix. Its eigenvalue expression is `lambda^2/(s2^(-1)+lambda)<=lambda`. It follows that

    0<=H<=E Dg<=A I.

This does not commute Hessians at distinct points. The argument remains valid for the genuinely C2/non-C3 and noncommuting examples in the independent checker. At r=0 the conditional law and m are independent of z; the derivative is zero. At r=1 the reverse bridge is not being evaluated, while the continuous velocity endpoint is m_1=g.

## 3. Complete retained-current ledger

Substitution of the conditional moments into the affine bridge gives, with `c=r/t`, `d=Delta/t`, `v0=Delta/t^2`,

    a+br=c,
    b s2=d,
    b^2 s2+v=v0,
    E(Y_t|z)=cz-dm,
    Cov(Y_t|z)=v0 I-d^2 H.

Since `d^2=v0 Delta`, the covariance is at least `v0(1-A Delta)I`, hence at least `v0 I/2`. This is a full matrix statement.

All conditional moments exist by strong convexity. Addition of the independent Gaussian changes no cumulant of order k>=3, and scalar multiplication by b multiplies a cumulant tensor of order k by b^k. Therefore every such exact descendant is `b^k kappa_k(X|z)`. They need not vanish. The checker explicitly finds nonzero third and fourth conditional cumulants for an asymmetric nonlinear potential.

For `g(x)=Bx`, direct block conditioning using the original endpoint covariance `(I+B)^(-1)` independently verifies the source formulas

    m=r B(I+s2 B)^(-1)z,
    H=B(I+s2 B)^(-1).

Here the missing covariance `d^2 H` in the mean-only kernel is exact, and at (r,t)=(0,1) the mean-only covariance I differs from `(I+B)^(-1)`. This is already an order-B defect for fixed, non-small increments. No scalar-trace replacement can repair it. Quadratic Gaussianity explains why there are no higher-cumulant defects in that special case; it does not extend to nonlinear U.

## 4. Conditional positive gluing and its actual read set

Fix the exposed caller and every declared external label. Take a coupling of the complete M output to `m+G_ref` with conditional mean-square error at most the square of its stated W2 error (or an arbitrarily close coupling and then pass to the infimum). The marginal law of G_ref is standard Gaussian conditional on the exposed data. It can be independent of those data while remaining coupled to M. It is not required, or permitted by the port, to equal an executed internal Gaussian root.

Draw N independently of the **entire coupling**. The source's stage (5) then has reference

    cz-dm-dG_ref+sqrt(v0-d^2)N.

Its noise covariance is v0 I and its actual/reference difference is exactly `-d(M-m-G_ref)`. This proves (6), because `v0-d^2=v0(1-Delta)>=0`. Degenerate zero reserve is allowed. The executed stage reads only the complete M output, the exposed caller/parameters, and its new N.

The proof does not retain a source/clock bank or a source-zero carrier as an extra observer. For the separate reserve in (8),

    C_res=v0[I-Delta(I+H)]
         >=v0[1-Delta(1+A)]I

The hypothetical covariance-service port must mean a conditional **Gaussian-law** approximation to `N(0,C_res)`, on a complete bank independent of M given the exposed data. With that law port, conditional convolution and the triangle inequality give error at most `d epsilon_M+epsilon_res` relative to the Gaussian with exact mean and covariance (4). The expectation is under the actual entering-caller law. No conditional error is silently evaluated under a different ideal marginal.

## 5. The alternative SDE is exact at the density level

The OU marginal density admits the representation

    rho_r(x)=gamma(x) E_G exp[-U(rx+sqrt(1-r^2)G)] / E_G exp[-U(G)].

Differentiating in x, with only the C1 force, gives

    grad log rho_r=-x-rm_r(x).

The forward-OU equation under the time change r=e^(-tau) gives, for r>0,

    partial_r rho_r=-(1/r)[Delta rho_r+div(x rho_r)]
                   =div(rho_r m_r).

The r=0 endpoint follows by the continuous extension (or directly by differentiating the displayed density); the potential normalization is independent of r. For `b_r=-x-(1+r)m_r`,

    -div(b_r rho_r)+Delta rho_r
      =div(rho_r m_r).

Thus both the coefficient 1+r and the sqrt(2) noise in (9) are correct. There is no missing r, 1/r, or factor two.

The source imports a sufficient Lipschitz bound from the established velocity analysis. The newly proved H bound in fact gives the stronger `Lip(m_r)<=rA`. For a direct uniform growth check, the conditional law at x=0 has mode zero. Integration by parts and `q·g(q)>=0` give `E|q|^2<=D(1-r^2)`, hence `|m_r(0)|<=A sqrt(D(1-r^2))`. Together these yield a uniform linear-growth bound. Gaussian domination of the expectation formula gives continuity at r=0 and r=1, including `m_1=g`.

The drift is therefore measurable in time, uniformly globally Lipschitz in space, and of linear growth. The SDE has a unique finite-second-moment solution. Uniqueness for its Fokker-Planck equation identifies the displayed rho marginals, including mu at r=1. This argument is about the exact continuous SDE; it establishes no discretization-error estimate.

## 6. Frozen-force stage: constants, coupling, and scope

Freeze r,h and the complete service versions before execution, with h>0 and r+h<=1. Solving the linear `-x` part with the remaining force frozen gives

    c_h=e^(-h),
    d_h=(1+r)(1-c_h),
    q_h^2=1-c_h^2-d_h^2.

    q_h^2 >= (1-c_h)(1+c_h-4(1-c_h))

Consequently `h<=log(5/3)` is a valid uniform sufficient condition. It is not asserted optimal after imposing r+h<=1. For h<=1/4, `c_h>=1-h>=3/4` and `1-c_h>=h e^(-h)>=3h/4`; hence

The output difference is exactly `-d_h(M-m-G_ref)`, giving the claimed `d_h epsilon_r(x)` conditional cost.
