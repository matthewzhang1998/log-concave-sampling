# Conditional innovation localizes the full F2 covariance gate

2026-10-04. New analytical reduction, independent review requested. This is a target localization, not an admitted finite VALUE producer. It responds constructively to the self-covariance separator without dropping its large term.

## Result

Let X_t, t>=0, be the Gaussian OU history started at X0=Z (so X_t corresponds to the multiplicative-clock X_(exp(−t))). Put

    I_t=integral_0^infinity e^(−u)g(X_(t+u))du,
    j(x)=E[g(x−I_0)|X0=x],
    e(x)=j(x)−g(x),
    R_t=g(X_t−I_t)−j(X_t),
    H_f=integral_0^infinity e^(−t)f(X_t)dt,
    S=integral_0^infinity e^(−t)R_t dt.

Then the exact second-substitution force is

    F2=H_j+S,  E2=F2−H_g=H_e+S.                      (1)

The centered innovation has a much smaller covariance than the whole chord:

    E[S|Z]=0,
    0<=Cov(S|Z)<=A4 I/8                              (2)

uniformly in Z. Consequently, in L2(Z;HS),

    Cov(E2|Z)=Cov(H_e|Z)+O(A4 sqrt(D)).               (3)

The potentially large order-A3 sqrt(D) self-covariance has therefore been localized to the deterministic conditional-mean field e along the true Markov path, rather than discarded.

There is also an explicit additional cubic current. Define

    K(x)=Cov(I_0,g(x−I_0)|X0=x),
    v_j=integral_0^1 P_r j dr, B_j=Dv_j,
    R2 f=integral_0^1 r P_r f dr.

With Sym(M)=(M+M*)/2,

    Cov(F2|Z)=2 R2[B_j B_j*](Z)
                      +2 Sym R2[K](Z)+O_L2HS(A4 sqrt(D)). (4)

The K-current is generally nonzero and order A3. The first term includes the previously missed self-covariance of H_e. These are precise candidate native targets. Neither j nor K is declared a strong oracle; their finite VALUE realization is still required.

## 1. Actual field bounds

Condition on X0=x and write X_u=e^(−u)x plus its OU Gaussian residual. The conditional path derivative with respect to x satisfies

    D_x I_0=integral_0^infinity e^(−2u)Dg(X_u)du,
    ||D_x I_0||op<=A/2.

Differentiating once under expectation is valid under the C2 Hessian bound, and gives

    Dj(x)=E[Dg(x−I_0)(I−D_x I_0)|X0=x].             (5)

The leading Dg term is symmetric. Thus

    ||Dj||op<=A(1+A/2),
    ||Dj−Dj*||op<=A2,
    ||De||op<=2A+A2/2,
    |e(x)|<=A E[|I_0||x]
           <=C A2(|x|+sqrt(D)).                     (6)

The last amplitude bound does not improve the full/captured-x first. It is the same issue exposed by the counterexample. The field j need not be a gradient. It has a controlled curl, but (5) is an analytical identity and not a permitted Hessian-valued producer.

## 2. The innovation martingale and its exact covariance constant

Let F_t be the filtration generated by Z and the OU Brownian motion up to time t. Markov conditioning gives E[R_t|F_t]=0. For s>=t, the Brownian Malliavin derivative of I_t is

    D_s I_t=sqrt(2) integral_(s−t)^infinity
                e^(−u)e^(−(t+u−s))Dg(X_(t+u))du,
    ||D_s I_t||op<=A e^(−(s−t))/sqrt(2).             (7)

Since X_t is fixed against future noise,

    D_s g(X_t−I_t)=−Dg(X_t−I_t) D_s I_t,
    ||D_s g(X_t−I_t)||op<=A2 e^(−(s−t))/sqrt(2).    (8)

Conditional Clark–Ocone on the future Brownian increments yields

    R_t=integral_t^infinity
          E[D_s g(X_t−I_t)|F_s]dB_s.

All integrands are matrix-valued original first-derivative analysis; no second derivative of g is used. Finite-time smooth approximations, the ordinary Gaussian Sobolev closure, and the displayed square-integrable deterministic majorants justify the formula under the admitted C2 class.

Stochastic Fubini now gives

    S=integral_0^infinity L_s dB_s,
    L_s=integral_0^s e^(−t)
                  E[D_s g(X_t−I_t)|F_s]dt,
    ||L_s||op<=A2 s e^(−s)/sqrt(2).                  (9)

Conditioned on Z, Ito isometry and integral_0^infinity s2 e^(−2s)ds=1/4 prove (2). In particular ||Cov(S|Z)||HS<=A4 sqrt(D)/8. This is a martingale-integrand bound. It is NOT a claim that the full same-tape map S has first O(A2) in the captured endpoint or all old Gaussian roots.

## 3. Cross terms involving the small-energy field e

The conditional energy of H_e obeys

    ||H_e||_(L2|Z=z)<=C A2(|z|+sqrt(D)),              (10)

using (6), each Gaussian row's conditional moment, and positive integral weights. Any centered H_e has a conditional Brownian martingale representation with square-integrated HS energy equal to its variance. Pair that representation with (9). For every HS-unit matrix M, Cauchy–Schwarz and ||M L_s||HS<=||L_s||op give

    |Cov(H_e,S|z):M|
       <=[E|H_e−E(H_e|z)|2]^(1/2)
                     [integral ||L_s||op2 ds]^(1/2)
       <=C A4(|z|+sqrt(D)).                          (11)

Thus Cov(H_e,S) and its transpose are O_L2HS(A4 sqrt(D)). Combining (1), (2), and (11) proves (3). This argument uses one physical energy, not the product of two sqrt(D) energies.

## 4. The remaining mixed innovation current is exact

The same estimate for H_g gives only O(A3 sqrt(D)); that term cannot be dropped. It has a simple exact conditional identity. Fix t and decompose

    H_g=integral_0^t e^(−u)g(X_u)du+e^(−t) I_t.

The first summand is F_t-measurable and E[R_t|F_t]=0. Also E[R_t|Z]=0. Therefore

    Cov(H_g,R_t|Z)
       =e^(−t) E[Cov(I_t,g(X_t−I_t)|X_t)|Z]
       =e^(−t) P_(e^(−t))K(Z).

Integrating with S's weight e^(−t) proves the exact identity

    Cov(H_g,S|Z)=R2 K(Z).                            (12)

At fixed x, the conditional Gaussian private first of I_0 is at most A, and the private first of g(x−I_0) is at most A2. Gaussian covariance/Poincare hence give

    ||K(x)||op<=A3,
    ||K(x)||HS<=C A3 sqrt(D).                        (13)

The bound is uniform in x after centering: the relevant conditional energies follow from Gaussian Poincare, not the unbounded deterministic conditional mean of I_0.

For any deterministic C1 Lipschitz vector field f, the OU martingale representation of H_f gives the general square identity

    Cov(H_f|Z)=2 R2[(Dv_f)(Dv_f)*](Z),
    v_f=R1 f.                                       (14)

Unlike a specialized gradient square, (14) uses the product with the transpose; no symmetry of Dv_f is assumed. Apply this with f=j. Then use H_j=H_g+H_e in the cross term Cov(H_j,S), invoke (11)-(12), and retain (2). This proves (4).

## 5. Quadratic calibration

For g(x)=a x in one dimension,

    j(x)=a(1−a/2)x, e(x)=−a2 x/2,
    B_j=a/2−a2/4, K=−a3/4,
    S=a2(H0/2−J0).

Using the exact conditional covariance matrix in the companion,

    Var(S|Z)=a4/8,
    Cov(H_g,S|Z)=−a3/8=R2 K,
    2 R2[B_j2]+2 R2 K=a2/4−a3/2+a4/16.

The true Cov(F2|Z)=a2/4−a3/2+5a4/16 differs by a4/4, exactly the remaining 2 Cov(H_e,S)+Var(S). Thus the constant in (2) is attained, and the cubic K-current is indispensable even on quadratics.
