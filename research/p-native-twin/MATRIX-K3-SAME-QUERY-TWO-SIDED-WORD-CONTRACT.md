## 2. What symmetric partial blocks supply

## 4. Exact two-sided word if its common-query jets are supplied

At the coefficient level, reversing a single factor word requires no new analytical derivative:

    [Ht(t_i) M H0(x_i)]*=H0(x_i) M* Ht(t_i).            (7)

The queries in (7) are the SAME t_i,x_i. Only order and the known row M are reversed. For B=ra E[K0-K1], an independently banked realization of B B* would average the four words

    (ra)^2 sum_(i,j=0,1) (-1)^(i+j)
       Ht(t_i) M H0(x_i) H0(x'_j) M* Ht(t'_j),          (8)

where primed/unprimed COMPLETE fine records are independent conditional on the same coarse X. Within either record, its terminal and ancestor query must stay jointly coupled. A B^2 executor instead has Ht(t'_j) after H0(x_i), and is not (8).

There is also a shorter skew-block formulation covering both terms of (4). Let P_S=[I,0,0], C=[cI,sI,0], L=[P_S*,C*], and

    D_i=diag(Ht(t_i),H0(x_i)),
    J_M=[0,M;-M*,0].

Then ||L||<=sqrt(2), ||J_M||<=1, and

    L D_i J_M D_i L*
      =P_S* K_i C-C* K_i* P_S.                         (9)

The difference of (9) for i=0 and i=1 is the pointwise leading curl divided by ra. After the SAME common fine-record expectation, it is C_lead/(ra) from (3). A supplied joint gradient-block channel for D_i, composed twice with known J_M and its known Gaussian fill, can therefore realize the selected skew word. The existing positive skew fork then keeps its whole stationary side body and uses the intact E marker; subtracting two channel VALUES does not by itself establish an executed kappa e mark.

One fixed left/right adapter cannot transpose an arbitrary two-factor word uniformly. That would require AB=BA, contradicted by (6)'s factors. This narrow observation does not exclude dimension-dependent transpose constructions or the factor-by-factor reversal (7).
