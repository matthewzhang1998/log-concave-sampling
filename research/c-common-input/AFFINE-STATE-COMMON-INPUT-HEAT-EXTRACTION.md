# Exact common-input heat extraction for a known affine state

## Status and scope

New bounded construction following the primitive-heat counterexample. This is an exact coefficient/source identification for a known affine query map with a nonlinear original potential. It is not yet a complete native covariance packet or a proof for a nonlinear ancestor q(Y). The positive fork, numerical clock provider, retained host and old-root endpoint contracts must still be supplied where used.

Let g=grad V have first at most1. Let D be a known symmetric contraction, B=I+aD, 0<a<1/2, so B and B^(-1) have fixed numerical norm bounds. Consider the SAME complete source

    E(W)=r[g(BW)-g(W)].

Known affine state/output arithmetic and both actual original-gradient calls are retained. Write kappa0=ra. Its curl is

    Curl E(W)=kappa0[H(BW)D-DH(BW)],       H=Dg.

## 1. Whole-source OU target, with exact correlation

Let c_t=exp(-t), v_t=sqrt(1-c_t²) and E_t=P_tE. Define

    H_t^B(Y)=E_Z H(B(c_tY+v_tZ)).

Then heat commutation gives EXACTLY

    Curl E_t(Y)=c_t kappa0[H_t^B(Y)D-DH_t^B(Y)].          (1)

The second terminal r g(W) is a genuine gradient and its OU heat remains a gradient. No separately heated ancestor is substituted in(1).

For the marked Riesz factor,

    R_(E_t)(Y)=D N^(-1)E_t(Y)
      =integral_(u>=0) c_(t+u) J_(t+u)(Y)du,
    J_s(Y)=E_Z DE(c_sY+v_sZ).                            (2)

Consequently the true orientation of the smoothed WHOLE source is

    O_(E_t)=-c_t kappa0 integral_(u>=0) c_(t+u)
                Sym E_Y[J_(t+u)(Y)
                      (H_t^B(Y)D-DH_t^B(Y))]du.          (3)

Both factors retain the SAME Y. Their auxiliary averaging variables may be independent conditional on Y, exactly because(3) is a product of the two specified conditional expectations. This is a source identity, rather than a marginal-law guess.

The earlier whole-source OU lemma restores O_E from O_(E_t) with error at most kappa e sqrt(2t), preserving the actual E mark.

## 2. A genuine-gradient VALUE source for the unmarked heat matrix

At fixed Y define

    h_Y(x)=(rho/v_t) B*[g(B(c_tY+v_t x))-g(c_t B Y)].      (4)

This is a genuine gradient in x: it is the gradient of
rho V(B(c_tY+v_t x))/v_t² minus its displayed constant linear term. Its full x-first is at most rho||B||². Its selected averaged Jacobian is

    E Dh_Y =rho B* H_t^B(Y) B.                           (5)

All queries in(4) are original gradient VALUES at their literal arguments. Thus the existing gradient pair applies to the small-radius source(4), with its own finite order, radius guard and numerical floor. The known matrices B^(-1), B^(-*) recover H_t^B from the selected matrix in(5). Their fixed norm bounds are charged to the input/output contractions and numerical readouts in the known joint adapter. No unknown matrix is inverted.

The actual old-Y source derivative is bounded by

    2rho c_t ||B||²/v_t.

This is a derivative of the executed VALUE graph. It is not a bound on D_Y H_t^B obtained by treating a Hessian as an oracle. Physical exterior callers use the corresponding actual center row, with the same rho/v_t factor. Known Gaussian fills and their arithmetic are charged; a cheap native implementation needs the actual scalar-block row representation or another certified fill routine.

There is an explicit block-skew realization. Put Hprime=B*H_t^B B, D_block=diag(Hprime,D), and L_B=[B^(-*),B^(-*)]. Since B=I+aD is symmetric and commutes with D,

    L_B D_block [0 I;-I 0] D_block L_B*
      =B^(-1)(Hprime D-D Hprime)B^(-1)
      =H_t^B D-DH_t^B.                                  (5a)

The first gradient block is(4); the second is the KNOWN linear gradient rho D x. This second block needs no new original-gradient oracle. Two independent complete block-pair banks use identical Y,B,t and finite versions. Normalize the known endpoint rows by a fixed bound on ||L_B|| and restore that numerical factor; known fills remain explicit. All B inverse norms are numerical for a<1/2, so there is no inverse protected-width label path. Equation(5a) is a literal instance of the supplied stationary skew/fork adapter. It provides the exact heat matching absent from the invalid additive g(BY+sZ) shortcut.

## 3. The intact marked source

For each u in(2), use the actual finite VALUE source

    f_(Y,t+u)(x)=[E(c_(t+u)Y+v_(t+u)x)
                            -E(c_(t+u)Y)]/v_(t+u).       (6)

Its x-first/curl are the original first/curl of E. Its averaged Jacobian is J_(t+u)(Y), exactly. The same whole E graph, its two terminal calls and all anchors are reevaluated at each displayed argument. The marked pair selected coefficient is c_sel J_(t+u), as required by the fork.

The conditional centered source energy, integrated over Y, is at most e/v_(t+u), by conditional variance and the standard marginal of cY+vZ. No energy of either unmarked terminal replaces e. The actual old-Y caller is priced through(6), including its anchor. Any finite clock rule must keep these width denominators and its true positive weights.

At positive t the minimum original width is v_t, not zero. The continuous weight in(2) has finite integral and the energy-weighted integral

    integral_0^infinity c_(t+u)/v_(t+u) du <= pi/2.

Thus there is no energy divergence as t tends to zero. This identity alone does not specify a finite numerical rule; its positive nodes, errors, fixed-order guards and complete query bill remain a real provider obligation. A matched dyadic rule also has the familiar logarithmic squared-frame sum, but that source rule must be printed before an executed full program is claimed.

## 4. What this resolves and what it does not

The affine-state common-input coefficient genealogy is exact even for a highly nonlinear original g. It preserves the same root, original VALUE versions, forward marked coefficient and genuine-gradient unmarked source. The counterexample's shell inflation disappears because the query is B(cY+vZ), with B applied to the WHOLE common Gaussian substitution.

For a nonlinear q(Y), simply replacing B by Dq(Y) in(4) is not a finite VALUE construction: it both invokes a derivative readout and omits its moving-root paths. Replacing it by an independently heated primitive changes the joint query law. The affine proof therefore identifies the precise additional structure needed for a nonlinear extension; it does not provide that extension.

At a later positive use, the fork must still retain any unmarked stationary body with its ordinary profile, supply the actual kept-center/protected-terminal certificate, and cancel or control its common-root covariance field at the SAME endpoint. The exact coefficient identity does not dispose of those descendants.
