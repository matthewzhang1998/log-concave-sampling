\begin{abstract}
We give a source-relative proof of the exact dimension exponent $1/32$ for finite original-gradient and directional Hessian--vector sampling in the $C^2$ Hessian-sandwich model. The modification is confined to the mixed coarse fourth packet. Two finite rectangular covariance reserves and a symmetric VALUE twin remove its leading rank-two defect at one common endpoint. Two compensated bilinear switches then return all four rooted four-tree shapes to the original compensated even core. Every new lower-rank descendant is retained at its actual executed amplitude.

At proxy mark $J_4=12/5$ and fourth upper heat $u_*=u_0a$, the complete interior, ordinary-prefix and final grades are $(16,14,18)$. The pure fourth body has the strictly positive exponent $1/30$. Together with the pinned raw order-seven source, accurate VALUE order-eight provider and original outer reduction, this yields
\[
 N_{\rm query}\le C L^C\left\{\kappa^2+\kappa^{17/16}
                  (D/\varepsilon^2)^{1/32}\right\}.
\]
The RAW7, retained/proxy, entire-tail, cap, covariance and accurate8 arguments are included here. The remaining source-relative boundary is the explicitly identified LOW30/LOW31 foundational constructors. This is a terminal law theorem with original-query accounting; it does not claim a differentiable return for the new packet or an unbounded-order sampling family.
\end{abstract}

\section{The ideal covariance mixture has no anisotropic width loss}
\label{sec:field-mixture}
We first prove the covariance-field estimate used by the matched-width raw construction. The estimate concerns its marginal law and preserves the full joint query array.

\begin{proposition}[A width-independent covariance-field estimate]
\label{prop:field-mixture}
Let $D,H:\R^n\to\R^d$ have first radii at most $\LL A$, and
$\norm{D-\E D}_2\le\LL\sqrt D A^{1+x}$. For each positive covariance
clock let $R_j=\sqrt{t_j}X_j$, $\sigma_j^2=1-t_j$, and let
$T_j$ be deterministic and self-adjoint, with $0\preceq T_j\preceq I$.
Within each clock, $X_j,W_j$ and the fresh averaging variables are
independent standard Gaussians. Use the actual field
\[
 S=\sum_jw_j\Sym\bigl\{\bar J_D(R_j)
                         \bar J_H(R_j,W_j)^T\bigr\}\,,
\]
where the two averages have Gaussian covariances $\sigma_j^2I$
and $\sigma_j^2T_j^2$, and the second is centered at
$R_j+\sigma_j\sqrt{I-T_j^2}\,W_j$.
If $\sum_jw_j/\sigma_j\le\LL$, then
\[
 \norm{S-\E S}_{L^2(\HS)}\le\LL\sqrt D A^{2+x},
 \qquad \norm S_{\op,\infty}\le\LL A^2\,.
\]
The centered matrix tests have subexponential scale $\LL A^2$,
independently of the least eigenvalue of $T_j$. Consequently, the
gapped covariance-mixture comparison of [30], \src{b27:calc:channel},
costs at most $\LL\sqrt D A^{4+x}$ between the unconditional laws
with covariances $vI-S$ and $vI-\E S$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a single clock, independently introduce $Z_1,Z_2$ and put
\[
 X=R+\sigma Z_1,\qquad
 Y=R+\sigma\sqrt{I-T^2}\,W+\sigma TZ_2\,.
\]
Conditional on $(R,W)$, the two Jacobians are independent, and
\[
 \E\bigl[\Sym\{JD(X)JH(Y)^T\}\mid R,W\bigr]
       =\Sym\{\bar J_D(R)\bar J_H(R,W)^T\}\,.
\]
Unconditionally, $X,Y$ are standard Gaussian and
$\Cov(X,Y)=tI$. This joint law is independent of $T$.
The mixed-source version of [30], \src{b27:calc:matrix-test},
therefore applies to the product with scale $C A^2/\sigma$.
To obtain that version, condition on a common isotropic root, expand
each Jacobian about its conditional mean, and apply the single-Jacobian
matrix test successively to the two centered factors. For the product
of the two mean Jacobians, the first-chaos bound gives derivative
$C A^2\norm K_{\HS}/\sigma$ for a matrix test $K$; Gaussian
concentration completes the conditional expansion. This uses only
the two first radii, so the two functions need not coincide.

Apply generalized H\"older with weights proportional to
$w_j/\sigma_j$, and then conditional Jensen given every actual
$(R_j,W_j)$. For symmetric $K$ the resulting bound is
\[
 \log\E\exp\bigl\{\lambda\langle K,S-\E S\rangle\bigr\}
 \le C\LL^2\lambda^2 A^4\norm K_{\HS}^2,
 \qquad |\lambda|\LL A^2\norm K_{\op}\le c\,.
\]
The change to an isotropic common-root representation was used only
for the unconditional product law, before Jensen. It did not replace
the actual law conditional on $W$.

For the Hilbert--Schmidt energy, first-chaos Bessel gives
$\norm{\bar J_D(R_j)}_{L^2(\HS)}
\le\norm{D-\E D}_2/\sigma_j$; the other factor has operator
norm at most $\LL A$. Sum using $\sum w_j/\sigma_j\le\LL$.
The operator bound is immediate. The cited gapped matrix-mixture
Stein estimate is the product of this Hilbert--Schmidt energy and
the matrix-test scale, giving $\LL\sqrt D A^{4+x}$.
This comparison integrates all internal clock roots. It neither
asserts a conditional comparison at each root nor improves a first
derivative of the executed return.
\end{proof}
