## 1. Common-reference assembly is essential and is now explicit

A total-cumulant heat bound controls the sum of coefficient tensors. It does not by itself control the sum of Hermite fields evaluated at unrelated independent reference Gaussians. For example, two local coefficients can have zero summed current tensor while their independent Hermite fields have positive energy.

The final note avoids this gap. It sets H=L P equal to the entire new source-zero physical Gaussian row, with fixed covariance C≥v0 I independent of the coefficient bank. All groups are compared simultaneously along

    W_s = W_old + sum_h X_h(s rho_h)
                  + (1−s) E_sum(B,H) + untouched keep,

where E_sum uses the summed main-current tensor and the covariance-C degree-seven Hermite score. At s=0 every group's root-zero property gives exactly H, so the endpoint is the common-H reference. At s=1 it is the native output. Every intermediate law is an actual positive pushforward.

This uses the finite vector-root extension of the imported *current-level* proof, rather than a sum of isolated Wasserstein estimates. The base-Gaussian first currents cancel at the same endpoint after summing. A remaining intrinsic branch has one root factor from the path derivative and a second from a nonlinear endpoint defect. The latter may belong to a different group. Hence the safe charge is

    Gamma_mix (sum_h Gamma_h |rho_h|)^2 sqrt(D),

or its Cauchy bound N Gamma_mix sum_h Gamma_h² rho_h² sqrt(D). No independence-based variance reduction is needed or asserted. All readout, cross-readout, finite moment, covariance-gap and node-count factors belong in Gamma_mix and Gamma_h. H is a known linear row of the owned dynamic bank; it need not be independent of that bank. This introduces fixed covariance transforms but no extra original private width in the frozen-coefficient block.

## 2. The exact first-Riesz identity

Use an explicit product primitive-bank representation in which B is standard Gaussian independent of the remaining old primitive tapes, the new reference H and the untouched keep. Let F(B,H,Y) be centered in B at each fixed H,Y. Let R_B=D_B N_B^(−1). At the common endpoint W_t=old+mean main+tF+keep, Gaussian integration by parts gives

    E[F · grad phi(W_t)]
      = E[(R_B F)(D_B W_old+t D_B F)^T : D² phi(W_t)].

No second derivative of the old finite program or original g is required. Centering is an orthogonal projection in B, so the centered field's L² energy is no larger than the uncentered main-field energy; no factor two is needed. The B-derivative of its B-mean is zero in this product representation.

If retained nonlinear observers depend on B, use a joint test, embed F in the physical coordinates and replace the old Jacobian by the full stacked endpoint Jacobian. Thus only a physical keep is required for this joint-current bound, but the entire stacked operator norm must fit the old budget. A B-independent observer contributes zero. An unbudgeted or exposed copy of B inserts an unsmall derivative and destroys the claimed old-first gain.

This is a joint-law statement. It does not assert that B remains Gaussian after conditioning on arbitrary nonlinear retained observations.

## 3. Why the explicit transfer factor is correct

At fixed B,Y, the matrix-valued R_B F is a Gaussian polynomial in the new physical reference variables of degree at most seven. Hilbert-valued Gaussian hypercontractivity gives

    ||R_B F||_(L²(B); L⁴(H))
       ≤ 3^(7/2) ||R_B F||_(L²(B,H))
       ≤ 3^(7/2) ||F||_(L²(B,H)).

The final inequality is the Gaussian Riesz L² contraction, applied to the coefficient Hilbert space. Suppose the old stacked and new Jacobians have operator L⁴ bounds uniform in B, at each fixed Y. Conditional Hölder and the matrix inequality ||A J^T||HS≤||A||HS ||J||op then give one Hilbert energy:

    ||current||2 ≤ 3^(7/2) E_main (L_old alpha + t L_B,main).

All coefficients in this current are independent of the untouched keep. Transferring one physical test derivative through a Gaussian keep of covariance at least kappa I costs at most 1/sqrt(kappa). Gaussian isometry introduces no physical-dimension factor. Integrating t∈[0,1] permits the conservative constant

    C_(7,kappa) = 3^(7/2)/sqrt(kappa).

The required uniformity is substantive. A coefficient estimate merely in L²(Y) and a Jacobian estimate in jointly averaged L⁴(Y,other tapes) do not justify this step. The final note now first fixes Y, uses B-uniform conditional L⁴ bounds with the same constants and then integrates Y. Alternatively one would need a separate product-integrability theorem.
