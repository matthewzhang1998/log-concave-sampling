2026-10-05. Audited files and exact hashes are recorded in `AUDITED-INPUT-SHA256.json`. This report does not edit or replace the author's main sources.

**PASS for the analytic source layer and marked-tree tensor composition, with a genuinely conditional native-admission ledger.** The source has the advertised same-center origin, active-probe parity, Gaussian response, dimension-sharp energy/cut bounds, radial gradient preservation, and actual private/probe/caller first bounds. The new all-cut theorem composes tensor bounds; it does not compose nonlinear native programs or close their law currents. Clarifications raised during this audit were incorporated into the main note: the smoothed tree bound starts at n≥2; the resolvent quadrature allowance is standard-retained-caller L², not uniform in arbitrary callers; numerical absolute response floors do not prove numerical Sobolev/curl control; radial, VALUE, and scalar errors share one local response budget; and the native return port must supply its complete extra inverse-width exponent, rather than silently interpreting dimension-sharp as width-uniform.

## 1. Same-center anchoring, parity, and executed cost

For each signed summand, both original-g queries use the same captured center. Consequently the exact smooth field vanishes at x=0 before integration. The normalized sign average has total absolute coefficient mass one, so two VALUE errors per summand yield 2 delta_g/(A t), independently of k. There are exactly 2^(k+1) raw calls in the uncached implementation.

Replacing any one probe by its negative permutes the sign bank and negates the source. This proves zero mean only while that probe is still independently symmetric in the relevant integration. It is not a conditional mean-zero theorem after that probe is exposed, tilted, or allowed to change the captured caller. The independent tests include explicit nonzero k=0 private means and nonzero k=1 private means conditional on an exposed probe.

For k≥1, a linear g cancels in the sign average, so the exact entire field vanishes. Floating-point cancellation is only a diagnostic and may leave a finite floor. The code correctly labels itself non-certified floating arithmetic.

The final production implementation applies the radial Jacobian as a scalar identity plus a rank-one action in O(D), with no dense identity/Jacobian allocation. Its O(k 2^k D+D) arithmetic and O((k+1)D) live-storage counts are correct, excluding original oracle work. The dense radial helper is diagnostic-only.

The production implementation is a faithful realization of the mathematical formula for correctly shaped finite inputs and a deterministic exact g VALUE function. It is not a general defensive numerical API: it does not promise interval arithmetic, rejection of all malformed/nonfinite data, or certified firsts for an arbitrary rounding oracle.

## 2. Exact Gaussian response and dimension-sharp constants

Differentiating first in X and then once in each independent P_j gives one factor a t per bank. The product of differentiation signs cancels the original Rademacher weight. The anchor vanishes from the X response. The total variance is exactly (k+1)a²t²=t², giving

    T_k = a^(k+1) t^k A^(-1) E[D^(k+1)g(z+tZ)].

No high derivative is queried. Weak Gaussian derivatives are legitimate because the original g has bounded first derivative and at most linear growth. Gradient symmetry makes the resulting response fully symmetric.

For each output coordinate, the products of one coordinate from every independent bank are orthonormal. Coordinatewise Bessel followed by summation over the single vector output yields ||T_k||HS≤a sqrt(D). This uses one energy, not one factor of sqrt(D) for every tensor slot.

For a p|q cut, retain one index on each side on Dg and transfer the other p−1 and q−1 indices to independent half-heat banks. The vector-valued Hermite score norms are at most sqrt((p−1)!) and sqrt((q−1)!). Pointwise ||Dg||op≤A and independence of the two score banks give exactly

    a^(k+1) 2^(k/2) sqrt((p−1)!(q−1)!).

The largest factorial product is k!, attained at an extreme proper cut. This proof does not infer a self-trace bound. Independent deterministic Fourier integration confirms the derivative order and total heat, while non-coordinate-separable tensor fixtures check every proper flattening through response rank seven.

## 3. Radial pullback, actual firsts, and precision

The radial h has matching value, first derivative, and second derivative at both seams. Its tangential eigenvalue h/r and radial eigenvalue h′ lie in [0,1]. A direct bilinear Hessian formula gives a bound smaller than the claimed 8/R; the latter is safely conservative.

Writing J=D psi, differentiating J^T f(psi,P,z) gives a term J^T Df and a Hessian-of-psi term bounded by (8/R)(3aR/2)=12a. Thus private first≤13a, complete active/private first≤1+12a≤13, and caller first≤1/t. The scalar potential pulls back exactly, so private curl is preserved. A scalar cutoff would not give that conclusion.

The caller bound uses the Hessian sandwich: the difference of two symmetric matrices in [0,A I] has norm at most A. For disjoint caller coordinates with actual row matrix M, the joint guard is sqrt(13²+||M||²/t²) after pullback. If coordinates overlap, insert and add the actual rows; the square-root combination does not apply automatically.

With roots frozen, differentiation in t gives the stated bound

    |partial_t F| ≤ a[2|psi(x)| + (sum_j |P_j|²)^(1/2)]/t.

The square-sum of probes follows by averaging the signed shift before applying Cauchy–Schwarz. It does not certify derivatives of a sampled-clock encoding.

Outside |x|≤R, the difference is bounded by (5a/2)|x|. Gaussian concentration plus a fourth-moment Cauchy–Schwarz estimate gives the advertised exp(−s²/8) response floor. Bessel carries this L² field error to HS response error without an extra dimension factor. The required R still contains sqrt(D), and its dependence on tolerance is real preparation work.

The response-floor telescope is valid with one *total* local floor. The final note explicitly allocates one third each to radial, VALUE, and scalar errors. Absolute value-error control alone never implies that a discontinuous numerical evaluation has bounded firsts or zero curl; those remain separately certified implementation obligations.

## 4. Full clock endpoints after actual coefficient heat

The half-heat split t_i≥tau/sqrt(2) does not require enlarging the stated B_n. Here is an explicit verification that includes that factor rather than omitting it.

At a degree-d vertex write e=d−1. Undoing the active response normalization gives:

- local HS constant (e+1)^(e/2) t^(−e) A sqrt(D);
- local proper-cut constant at most 2^(e/2) sqrt(e!) t^(−e) A.

At t≥tau/sqrt(2), both are covered by 2^e e! tau^(−e), with the single sqrt(D) only in the HS bound. For the HS claim, (e!)²≥e^e≥((e+1)/2)^e for e≥1; the first inequality follows by pairing j with e+1−j. The e=0 case is immediate.

Since sum e=n−2 and product e!≤(n−2)!, the marked-tree product is bounded by B_n=2^(n−2)(n−2)!. Positive clock masses are at most one. Multiplying by S_n gives (9). The bound is uniform over every original structural and primitive clock; no endpoint cutoff is needed for these smoothed main coefficients. A union of at most 2n−1 endpoint sectors has the stated absolute mass upper bound (2n−1)delta times the uniform envelope. This is an upper certificate, not a claim of cancellation or zero tail.

The original target is changed. The Appell heat mismatch n! A^n tau sqrt(D) remains due, and the imported quadrature estimate is L² over the original standard Gaussian retained caller. Neither result establishes a joint law with retained coefficient banks or gives uncharged observer derivatives.

The source-preparation telescope uses one local HS error and proper cuts on all other factors. Its displayed n S_n 2^(2n)(n−2)! constant safely dominates the half-heat factors and δ≤1 perturbations. It gives an absolute response floor with no extra dimension multiplier, at the cost of the stated local accuracy and radius.

## 5. Marked-tree composition and its exact boundary

For a boundary input/output split, each tree-edge component and its complement contain a terminal because all graph-theoretic leaves are marked. Expanding its net balanced mass leaves cross-component input/output pairs. There is at least one such pair. Different pairs have distinct exponents 2^i+2^j because the global input and output sets are disjoint. A signed sum of distinct powers of two cannot vanish: its largest term exceeds the sum of all smaller ones.

Every internal edge therefore has a nonzero conserved orientation. With terminal input/output legs included, conservation makes every local tensor flattening proper. A directed underlying tree is acyclic, so the contraction is a composition of tensor products of bounded local maps and identity wires. This proves the full product-of-cuts bound.

The one-HS proof roots at the chosen HS tensor and attaches vertices outward. Each newly attached vertex still has a future tree edge or an external mark, so its local cut is proper. For the stated loopless multigraph extension, a spanning tree with marked leaves and a marked-leaf HS root permits all edges to the already built group to be contracted at each attachment. This proves only its one-HS conclusion. It does not prove cyclic all-cut control, handle a local self-trace, or supply a native return theorem.

Independent tests use recursive tree growth rather than the author's Prüfer generator, exact integer flow certificates, heterogeneous internal tensor dimensions, and loopless multigraphs retaining a marked-leaf spanning tree.

## 6. Conditional grade ledger and cost consequences

The following rational arithmetic is correct:

    tau=alpha^(1/n), beta=1/[2(n−1)],
    r_n=n−3/2+2/n,
    alpha^(n+1) tau^(−(n−1))=alpha^(n+1/n).

At n=8 the root amplitude grade is 27/4, root-caller grade 53/8, conditional zero-extra-loss quadratic grade 27/2, and joined/heat target 65/8. The root's own extra caller loss is explicitly present; nonroot caller paths have root attenuation plus their beta before their inverse tau.

A return prefactor tau^(−ell) changes the quadratic grade to 2r_n−ell/n. Thus rank eight has 27/2−ell/8, not automatically 27/2. Quadratic control in a norm containing both complete caller firsts would, for example, give 53/4. That still exceeds 65/8, but the complete port must state and charge every such exponent. At rank eight ell=43 spends the entire nominal quadratic surplus. The final note now explicitly requires ell≤n²−3n+3, includes ell and its constant in the missing port, and labels 27/2 as the zero-extra-loss case. Dimension-sharp control alone does not imply width-uniform control.

For main coefficient preparation or quadrature, amplification by tau^(−(n−2)) means a target alpha^(n+1/n) requires a dimensionless local floor of order alpha^((n−1)/n), up to the explicit rank/readout constants. At rank eight this is alpha^(7/8). A VALUE floor at minimum t then has scale A alpha, again before the wrapper's own precision demands. These requirements may improve logarithmic quadrature counts but do not price native filters or their small-radius dependence.

The independent degree-multiset census reproduces T_8=794,880, S_8=32,049,561,600, B_8=46,080, and V_8=27,020,800 raw VALUE calls over all unmerged histories at one clock tuple per history. The node grids, source radii, finite pair/filter order, selected normalization, absolute floors, caller/readout work, and every changed-version replay must still enter the full bill. A native comparison port must also certify all conditional, public-tilt, auxiliary, whole-old-bank and observer currents at the same endpoint; exact covariance gaps; finite means; actual derivative frames; and completed-terminal curl. Fixed-order local source existence and the source-level curl proof do not provide these results.
