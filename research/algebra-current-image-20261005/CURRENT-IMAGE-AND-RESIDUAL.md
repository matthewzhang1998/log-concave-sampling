## 1. Fixed endpoint and normalization

Use a scalar diagnostic endpoint

    X0 = W(E) + sqrt(v) H + sqrt(kappa) K,

with v,kappa>0. H and K are independent standard Gaussians independent of E and of any coefficient bank B not already in E. Neither H nor K is an exported observer. E includes the literal frozen geometry, force versions and original clock list. If B is retained, its equality scope is explicitly conditional; if B is consumed, that fact must be part of the requested current contract.

The notation j_r D^r means the observable pairing E[j_r phi^(r)(X0)]. For a retained E record, equality requires E[chi(E) sum_r j_r phi^(r)(X0)] to agree for every bounded measurable chi and every smooth compactly supported phi. Equivalently it is a conditional-on-E identity. For a consumed E record no such test is retained; that is a different quotient. There is no implicit r!. A cumulant tensor must be divided by r! before it is entered in this convention. The rank-zero normalization current remains in the general syntax, but all exact Gaussian perturbations computed here have zero mass current.

A finite logarithm in theta and a law current are different objects. A formal multiplier theta^r becomes the constant-coefficient operator D^r at X0. This is an exact identification of finite formal coefficients, not proof that the formal multiplier is the transform of a positive law. A native positive source has a separate row and floor.

## 3. A connected bridge and its whole conditional defect

Let two packets be the two-vertex marked paths (0,1) and (2,3). Each initially has k=(0,0), one internal edge and one physical mark per vertex. Their coefficient functions are F(B),G(B). A bridge has exactly four hit histories:

    (0,2), (0,3), (1,2), (1,3).

For hit (i,j), add that edge and raise k_i and k_j to one. The graph has N=4, K=2, M=3, R=4. All old physical marks and original clock weights remain. Each hit graph has raw source cost 2+2+4+4=12 VALUES per native source sweep, before wrapper repeats, cubature and replay.

With B+=sqrt(s)B+sqrt(1-s)U+ and B-=sqrt(s)B+sqrt(1-s)U-,

    Cov(F,G) = integral_0^1 E[F'(B+) G'(B-)] ds.

The derivatives use the original injection rows. The sqrt(s) factor of the retained-B representation is not substituted into these coefficient derivative slots.

Conditional on B, the produced coefficient is instead

    K(B) = integral_0^1 E_U[F'(B+) G'(B-)] ds.

Thus replacing a deterministic whole-bank covariance by this retained coefficient has the **entire leading defect** [K(B)-Cov(F,G)]D^4. It has zero complete bank mean; it need not vanish in the retained-record observable quotient.

For a concrete original gradient take g(x)=c x+epsilon sin x with 0<epsilon<c and c+epsilon<=A. At width t let d=epsilon exp(-t^2/2), so the heated C0 coefficient is c+d cos B. Take the two-force coefficient

    F(B)=G(B)=(c+d cos B)^2.

Its bridge conditional integrand is exactly

    [2cd exp(-(1-s)/2) sin(sqrt(s)B)
       +d^2 exp(-2(1-s)) sin(2sqrt(s)B)]^2.

Therefore K(0)=0, while Var(F)>0. The defect at B=0 is -Var(F). Since K is continuous, the defect remains nonzero on a neighborhood of zero and is detected by bounded retained-B tests; the obstruction does not rest on a null-set value. The checker verifies the complete integral identity symbolically using all four frequency pairs. This is a scope obstruction, not a topological one.

If the complete B bank is consumed and X0 has no B-dependent physical readout, the defect has zero leading pairing. If X0 or a retained test reads B, its vanishing cannot be inferred. The associated observer/endpoint branches remain mandatory.

## 5. One exact positive covariance/time branch

       + s^2[r D^2/2+q^2 D^4/2]

    d/ds E phi(X_s) = E[(q+s r) phi''(X_s)].

These are two descriptions of the same positive path; confusing their endpoints drops the D^4 branch. This row has a fully known first nonmain residual without using a native norm comparison.

## 6. Three critical overlap tests

### Differentiation and joining

There are two different derivative operations. For frozen widths, clocks and constant original injection rows, let D_z translate the independent occurrence centers before replica substitution. Let J_alg(F,G) be the unreplicated differential-product bridge. Then the exact coefficient identities are

    D_z J_alg(F,G)=J_alg(D_z F,G)+J_alg(F,D_z G),
    D_z^2 J_alg(F,G)=J_alg(D_z^2F,G)
                    +2J_alg(D_zF,D_zG)+J_alg(F,D_z^2G).

The checker expands every base bridge hit and all ordered additional occurrence-center hit words through order two. Repeated hits at one occurrence are retained.

This does not commute unchanged with differentiation of the actual retained-B representation. Writing J_s=E_U[F'(B+)G'(B-)], the literal chain rule is

    D_B K(B)=integral_0^1 sqrt(s) E_U[F''G'+F'G''] ds,
    D_B^2 K(B)=integral_0^1 s E_U[F'''G'+2F''G''+F'G'''] ds.

The extra sqrt(s) or s is mandatory. A bridge derivative slot still has its original injection row; a later caller hit through B+ or B- has the new sqrt(s) row. Confusing the two produces REPLICA_RETAINED_ROW_CHAIN_FACTOR. The discrepancy is explicit and repairable by retaining the correct scalar row, rather than by assuming a commutation relation.

For F=G=c+d cos B, the node coefficient is d^2 exp(-(1-s)) sin^2(sqrt(s)B). At s=1/4, B=pi/2, its retained derivative is d^2 exp(-3/4)/2; the unweighted pre-replica derivative is d^2 exp(-3/4). The checker verifies this exact counterexample as well as the first two correct chain rules.

If the bridge also has live scalar row coefficient A(B)C(B), differentiating emits (A'C+AC')F'G', with its actual replica ancestry. Live covariance, widths, readout normalizations and source preparation parameters contribute their corresponding chain-rule branches. Omitting these is LIVE_INJECTION_OR_WIDTH_DERIVATIVE, not a proved commutation relation.
