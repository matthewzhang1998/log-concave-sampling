Under general C2 assumptions one cannot freely replace a literal VALUE heat loss O(tau) by O(tau^(1+epsilon)). For example take an absolutely summable positive sequence a_j and rapidly separated frequencies k_j, and

    g(x)=c x + sum_j (a_j/k_j) sin(k_j x),
    0<sum_j a_j<c,  c+sum_j a_j<1.

Then g' is continuous, in (0,1), and U is C2. At tau_j=1/k_j the jth Gaussian-heat loss has size `(1-e^(-1/2))a_j tau_j`; for example choose `c=1/2`, `a_j=1/(16j^2)`, and `k_j=exp(j^2)`. This is not bounded by any fixed power tau_j^(1+epsilon), even in standard-Gaussian L2: for positive frequencies, `E sin(k_i Z) sin(k_j Z) = [exp(-(k_i-k_j)^2/2)-exp(-(k_i+k_j)^2/2)]/2 >=0`, so the squared norm of the full heat defect is at least its jth squared diagonal term, `(1-exp(-1/2))^2 a_j^2 tau_j^2 [1-exp(-2k_j^2)]/2`. The ratio to tau_j^(2+2epsilon) diverges. This only rules out deriving the missing improvement from the bare VALUE regularity; it does not rule out extra cancellations or clock factors in a particular full cumulant.
